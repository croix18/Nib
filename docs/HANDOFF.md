# Nib — handoff

Read this before changing anything. It is the project memory: what Nib is, how it works, how it was trained and tuned, how to rebuild it, and where it is weak.

## What it is

Nib turns live handwriting (pen, finger or mouse strokes) into LaTeX, in the browser, with no network and no dependencies. It ships as one file, `dist/nib.js` (about 1.3 MB including the models, the typeset fonts and the canvas), so it can be pasted into any single-file tool (Deckhand, Cadence, Tally, Boards Up, the practice tests) and run from Drive over `file://` on the classroom Chromebox. `dist/Nib.html` is a self-contained tester with everything (fonts, KaTeX) inlined. Since 1.2 it can also show what it read in place — the writer's strokes tidied, re-drawn in their Replicator handwriting, or typeset where they were written — and `dist/Board.html` is a whiteboard built on that. Since 1.3 it can also work out any line that ends with "=" and write the answer after it, in the writer's own hand; since 1.4 it solves equations ("x =" under one), and it can treat x as a letter only (`times: false`, the Board's setting), with a raised dot or brackets for multiplication. 1.5 is the classroom-panel round: pen, finger and palm rules with two fingers at once, answers behind a tap-to-reveal chip, pages, save as image, a movable rail, settings, and the Algebra 1 solver (quadratics, rearranging a formula for a letter, repeating decimals). 1.6 makes the Board a canvas: an endless board that pans and zooms, pens with pressure, markers, highlighters, colors, shapes (number lines and coordinate planes among them), text, pictures, select, a laser, papers per page, PDF and picture export and a board file (see The canvas, below); Nib still reads only what the pen and marker write. 1.6.3 draws an answer waiting for a tap as a blur of itself where it goes (the gold "?" chip is gone), goes inside other tools — a fixed page and a layer of answers over a host's own ink (Inside another tool, below), which Deckhand's Sketch Pad and its pen over the slides now use — reads a 1 with a long, flat top as the 7 an American reader sees, and gives the Board's selection Deckhand's finger-sized frame and handles. 1.6.4 is a fix: the highlighter drew only the dot it began with (13 Nov 2026, Croix at the Promethean board: "The default pen or marker worked well. The highlighter broke. It made a dot and didn't track."), and a pen stroke that paused and carried on was drawn only to the pause — see Drawing caches, under The canvas. 1.6.5 scribbles out — a pen or marker scribbled back and forth (or round and round) over writing rubs out what it lies over, as one step of Undo (Croix, 8 Nov 2026: "Scribble out to erase", then "I've experienced other apps that claim scribble to erase works and it sucks. I want nib and by extension deckhand to work super well. Double check and harden it to make sure it's up to my standards."); see Scribbled out, below — and an answer keeps the look it was first drawn with (13 Nov 2026: "I like that nib adapts as I go, but I don't like it to constantly update the previous answers."; Answers after "=", 11). 1.6.6 hardens a scribble heard in pieces: the gap between the pieces is timed by the pointer events' own time stamps, so a page busy drawing between them (a full board on the classroom's Chromebox) does not make one scribble two strokes of ink — see Scribbled out, In pieces.

It reads ink, not photos. A photo of paper is a different (much harder) problem and is out of scope.

## The canvas (1.6)

**World and camera.** With `infinite` the pad is an endless board. Everything is kept in world units (CSS px at zoom 1); world point (x, y) is drawn at screen point ((x − cam.x)·z, (y − cam.y)·z), z from 0.1 to 8 (`setCamera`, `getCamera`, `panBy`, `zoomAt(f, sx, sy)`, `zoomTo`, `fitContent(m, most)`, `viewRect`, `toScreen`, `toWorld`; `nib:camera` at most every 100 ms). Drawing code gets a context already in world units. **The chosen view**: panning or zooming makes the view on screen the writer's (`_want`: a world rect and a zoom); a resize keeps the writing that was in it in sight from the same top-left corner, zooming out only as far as it must and back in when the screen grows again (`_fitView`; an empty view keeps its zoom); `getState().cam` is that view with the size of the screen it was chosen on (`{x, y, z, w, h}`), so a page opened on a smaller screen shows what it showed. Nothing on the page moves (1.5's `fit` scaled the ink itself; it is still there for pads without `infinite`).

**Elements.** An element is a points array, the same object for its whole life (history refers to it); its look is in `pad.info` (a `WeakMap`): `type` (the pointer), `width` (contact), `tool` ('pen' when absent, 'marker', 'highlighter', 'shape', 'text', 'image'), `color` (a named color like '@red' that has a shade for light and for dark paper, `PAD_COLORS` / `padColor`, or a CSS color; none: the paper's ink), `size` (line width, or a text's font size), `shape`, `fill` ('tint', 'solid' or a color), `dash`, `opt` (a number line's `{min, max, step, labels, frac}`, a plane's `{xmin, xmax, ymin, ymax, step, labels, grid}`), `text`, `img` (a picture's id). Points: ink `[x, y, t, p?]` (p: the pen's pressure, 0–1; the line's width is `size·(0.45 + 1.1·p)`), a shape's two drag points (a polygon its vertices), a text or picture its box's corners. **Nib reads only ink: pen and marker** (`pad.ink`; `read: false` reads nothing); highlighters, shapes, text and pictures never change a reading, and `getStrokes()` still hands out `[x, y, t]`. Drawn in this order: paper, pictures, highlighters (see-through: multiply on light paper), then everything else as made, the read ink in its view and the answers, the strokes being made, the chips, the eraser's ring, the selection and the laser.

**Input.** A pointer down picks its mode from `pad.mode`: 'draw' (with `pad.tool` = `{kind, color, size}`), 'erase', 'fix', 'select', 'pan', 'shape' (`pad.shapeTool`), 'text' (`pad.textTool`) or 'laser'; `setMode('lasso')` is 'select' with `lassoOnly` (the Board's Lasso tool: a drag always draws a lasso, with a mouse too, wherever it starts outside the selection; a tap picks one thing). Panning: the hand ('pan'), Space or the middle mouse button with a mouse (`spacePan`, set by the page), the wheel (Shift: sideways; Ctrl, or a trackpad pinch: zoom about the pointer), a finger with `fingers="pan"`, and two fingers put down together (within 0.3 s and a hand's width; the first finger's stroke is taken back) unless `pinch="false"`; fingers in a gesture never write. A second finger far from the first writes (two students). A gesture whose fingers have been quiet for 1.5 s lost a `pointerup` somewhere, and a pen ends a gesture of fingers: neither keeps the board from listening (and fingers quiet for 1.5 s are dropped from `touches`). A touch tap within 3 s of the pen makes no shape and no text box (the heel of the hand). **Draw and hold** (`hold`): a stroke held still 0.6 s, that `shapeRecognize` calls a shape, becomes that shape (a highlighter only a line) — only when it is bigger than the writing: a line or arrow at least 80 screen px and 4× `refSize` of the board's ink, anything else 48 px and 2.5× (with fewer than three ink strokes, the pixel sizes alone), so a 1, a 0 or a fraction bar held still stays writing. The pen moving on more than 6 screen px after the hold fired turns the stroke back into ink, all of it (8 Nov 2026: on Croix's tablet a pause mid-stroke made the stroke so far a line and dropped the rest — "my pen kept skipping"). **The eraser** takes ink, and shapes where it touches their outline; text, pictures, number lines and planes are placed things that writing goes on, and stay (`_erasable`). `eraser="area"` cuts ink where it rubs (`padCut`) and takes shapes whole. **Shapes** snap to `snap` (world units; the Board gives the paper's spacing). **Select**: a mouse drag from empty board draws a box, a pen or finger a lasso — also when it starts on a picture, plane or number line that is not selected, away from its edge (14 screen px), so the writing on a worksheet can be selected (a drag from the edge moves the picture; a box or lasso takes those only when they are wholly inside it); a tap selects one thing (in a group: just it); the frame stands 18 screen px off what it holds and is never under 96 px a side, with 22 px handles grabbed within 22 px of their centre by a finger or a pen (14 by a mouse) — Deckhand 7.60.1's frame, for the same complaint (1.6.3); handles on the corners and sides scale (a corner keeps the shape's proportions; text and pictures always do), a drag inside the frame or within 16 px of it moves; each is one step of history. In the Tidy, My hand and Typeset views the drawn layout goes with what is moved or sized at once (`_planOf` / `_planTo`), before the re-reading catches up. `select`, `selectAll`, `clearSelection`, `deleteSelection`, `copySelection` → `{v, items, box}`, `paste(clip, at)`, `duplicateSelection`, `restyleSelection({color, size, textSize, fill, dash, opt})`, `orderSelection(front)`, `nudgeSelection(dx, dy)`; `nib:select {count, box, kinds}`. **Text**: a tap with the text tool opens a textarea over the board (a tap on a text opens it; Escape or Ctrl+Enter or a tap elsewhere ends it; empty, it goes); `nib:edit`. **Pictures**: `addImage(src, {at})` (≤ 70% of the view, selected, one step; `nib:image {id, src}` for the page to keep it), `putImage(id, src)`, `imageIds()`, and `nib:needimage {id}` when one is drawn that the pad does not have. **The laser**: a red trail that fades over 0.7 s, never kept. History steps: add, erase (+ hide), ops (inserts and removals in order: the area eraser, paste, ordering), restyle, move (selection moves, nudges, a text fitted), clear, reveal.

**Answers on an endless board** are fitted to the view as the writer sees it when they are worked out (an answer that would run past its right edge is squeezed in, or written under its line); they do not move when the board is panned afterwards.

**Export.** `exportImage({rect, margin, scale, maxPixels, paper, background, sheet, answers, only, look})`: a canvas of a world rect (default: everything, with a margin) drawn as the board looks at 100% (`look`) at `scale` px per world unit (capped at `maxPixels` and 16,000 px a side), on the page's paper or on `sheet` (another paper, e.g. plain white, without touching the board), answers 'shown', 'all' (a key) or 'none', `only` some elements; `contentRect(m, {hidden})` (with `hidden`, the answers still waiting for a tap count too: an answer key's page is wide enough for them).

**State.** `getState()` adds, for the canvas, `meta` (per element its look, compact: `k` tool, `c` color, `s` size, `sh` shape, `f` fill, `d` dash, `o` opt, `x` text, `im` picture id; null for a plain pen stroke), `press` (per stroke, pressure × 100, or null), `cam` and `looks` (1.6.5: the look each answer was first drawn with, Answers after "=", 11); `setState` puts them back (a state without them is plain pen strokes, as before).

**The Board** (`board/board.js`). The tools are the pad's modes; each tool's color and size live in the Board (`S.styles`) and go to `pad.tool` / `shapeTool` / `textTool`; the pen's size is also the pad's `stroke-width` (answers are written with it). Each page has a paper, `{kind, tone}`, set as the pad's `paper` attribute (and `snap` from `SNAP`). Storage: `nib-board-v3` = `{v: 3, t (when), view, solve, reveal, rail, theme, finger, pinch, train, trainNoted, ink, styles, eraser, hold, snap, pressure, read, paper, xp (the export choices), page, pages: [{pk (each element's points packed by storePack), fixes, revealed, types, meta, cam, paper}]}`; a page not on the board is packed once and kept packed (`packed`, a `WeakMap`). 1.5 kept its board under `nib-board-v1`; when there is no `nib-board-v3` it is read from there (`v: 2`, points as arrays, `pen: thin/medium/thick`: its strokes are given the width they had; an older `{view, solve, strokes}` is page 1) and left as it was, so an older Board still opens what it had. A board saved by a newer Board (`v` above 3) is shown as far as this one can and nothing is saved (`newer`). **One Board saves**: the first tab takes the Web Lock `nib-board` and holds it while open; a second tab or window shows the board, saves nothing (board, training, drill, nor the picture clean-up) and says so, and when the lock comes free offers to reload (no Web Locks: a `storage` event says once that two are saving). Everything read from storage or a file goes through `okPage` / `okMeta` / `okStyle` / `okPaper`: a color must be a named color or `#rrggbb`, sizes and numbers are clamped, unknown kinds dropped. A save that fails is said, and the note stays (with Save the board) until a save works. A text box still open is committed before a page change, a save, an export or a clear (`commitText`). Pictures go to IndexedDB on `nib:image` and come back on `nib:needimage`; four seconds after the board opens, pictures no page uses are deleted — only when the board itself was read from storage, never one met this session (an undo can bring it back) or named by the board in storage at that moment (another tab), and only once it is a day old (the id carries its time: another tab may not have saved its page yet). **Export** draws the page on the board with the board's own pad and any other page with a second pad out of sight (`renderPad`, sharing the pictures, the board's size and the pen's pressure), waiting for each page's reading (and for its pictures) before drawing it; the second pad is used by one thing at a time (`withR`: an export and the pages overview's thumbnails take turns); a PDF is one JPEG per page at about 200 dpi on Letter or A4 (turned to suit the writing, 1/2 inch margins, never more than 1.5× its size on the board, the paper filling the sheet) or on a page the writing's own size (0.75 pt per px). The board file (`Nib-board-<date>.nibboard.json`; `.nibboard` opens too) is `{format: 'nib-board', version: 3, saved, engine, page, pages (as stored), images: {id: data URL}}`; it opens at its `page`, by Open a board or dropped on the board (anything else dropped that is not a picture is said and left). Published on claude.ai, files are saved through the `downloads` capability (`window.claude.use('downloads')`): the viewer is asked, and a refusal is said, never "Saved". Copied elements paste on any page; the clipboard is given a line of text marking the copy, so a paste of anything else (a picture, text from elsewhere — which becomes a text box) is told apart. Keys: V O H P M I E R T L (tools), S, X, F, 1–4, Delete, the arrows (nudge, or move the board), [ ], + −, Ctrl+Z / Y / C / X / V / D / A / 0 / 1, Space (pan), Escape.

**Drawing caches** (`_path`, `_varPaths`, `_bounds`). Each element's Path2D (or, for a pen with pressure, its pieces of one width) and its bounds are kept per element, keyed by the element, and are good for as many points as they were made from: a stroke being written is the same array growing (`P.cur`, which becomes the stroke itself on lift), so a cache whose count is not the stroke's is made again. Anything that changes points without adding any calls `_touched`. Until 1.6.4 the count was not checked: the highlighter, which is not drawn piece by piece as it goes (`_live` is false for it: see-through pieces would darken where they overlap) but whole on every frame, kept the drawing of its first frame — a dot — and so did a pen stroke the board redrew while it was being written (another pointer writing at once, an answer coming into focus, and a pause that made the stroke so far a shape before the pen moved on — 1.6.1's "my pen kept skipping": 1.6.1 kept the points, but the drawing still stopped at the pause), once the pen lifted. `test/canvas_browser.js` writes each tool at a hand's pace, and the highlighter with a finger too (the Promethean's frame reports touches) (`lineSlow`, a frame between moves; `line` sends every move at once, which is how the tests missed it).

## Inside another tool (1.6.3)

Deckhand (croix18/deckhand, `tools/vendor_nib.py` puts `dist/nib.js` in its file) uses two of these; both are attributes of `<nib-pad>`.

- **`page="W H"`** (`o.page`, implies `infinite` for the camera machinery): `_pageCam()` shows the page whole and centred, as big as the element allows — about the page's middle, with any ink beyond its edges taken in (`_bounds` of every stroke, 6 units of air), so a card made smaller never cuts off what was written in a wide card's margins. It runs only from `_resize`, `setOptions` (a new page) and `setState`, never while writing. With a page nothing else moves the camera: `_wheel`, `setCamera`, `panBy`, `zoomAt`, `fitContent` return at once, `_gestureDown` says no (two fingers write), `getState` leaves `cam` out and `setState` ignores one. Deckhand's Sketch Pad (1280 × 960, the size its old fixed canvas had) draws its background under the pad from `toScreen([0, 0])` / `toScreen([1280, 960])` and repaints on `nib:camera`.
- **`draw="answers"`** (`o.layer = 'answers'`): `_paint` leaves out the paper, the strokes and every read item but the answers, and returns before the live pointers; `_down` returns at once. The host gives its strokes with **`syncStrokes(list, moved)`**: `_hostMap` (a `WeakMap`) maps a host array to the pad's own cleaned copy, so a host array seen again is the same stroke — its sid, its corrections, the answers shown on its line (`revealed` keys are sids + answer); strokes no longer given go; no history. `moved` = `[[newArray, oldArray], …]`: the old stroke's points are replaced in place by the new array's and it is mapped to the new array (its sid stays), then `_inkMoved(…, true)` reads everything again (the recognizers' caches are by stroke identity, and a join probability between a moved stroke and an unmoved one would be stale). A stroke's points come from one host array at a time (`_hostSrc`): given an older array of it again — the host's Undo of a move hands back the arrays it had — its points are that array's again, in place (a review of Deckhand 7.61 found the answers left where a move had put them after its Undo). A pair whose points are the same (a recolor) moves nothing; a translation re-reads the parts (`parts.clear()`), a change of size also starts the recognizers again. Returns whether anything changed. **`answerAt(clientX, clientY)`** → `{key, hidden, box (client px)}`: a hidden answer anywhere on its target (`_chipAt`), a shown one on its own box; **`showAnswer(key)`** (as a tap would: `_reveal`) and **`hideAnswer(key)`**. Deckhand's pen over the slides gives its pen and marker strokes (each cut where a later eraser stroke passed — its eraser paints `destination-out`), asks `answerAt` on its own pointer down and calls `showAnswer` when that pointer lifts within 10 px and 400 ms.
- **`symbolAt(x, y)`** (world units): the symbol drawn under a point, the smallest one it is inside, else the nearest within 12 screen px — what the Board's Fix does, for a host's Fix (`nib:tap` → `symbolAt` → `relabel`).
- **For a tool that had a pad of its own** (1.6.3, for Deckhand's Sketch Pad): `palm="false"` (`o.palm`) turns off every pen-and-palm rule (`_wide`, the pen's second, `_brushed`, the young and still touches a pen drops, the pen session's heel taps and waiting brief touches, the text and shape tools' heel rule) — every touch writes; `single` (`o.single`): another pointer put down while one writes is ignored (with the palm rules on, a pen still takes over from a touch); `keep`: `disconnectedCallback` leaves the pad alive (no teardown), so the same element put back later is the same pad — history, corrections, revealed answers, reading; `setState(state, {steps: true})`: the history is an `add` per stroke (Undo takes them back last first); `setState` refits a page (`_pageCam`, margins included); `relabel` sends `nib:change` (and `onChange`), so a host that saves on changes keeps a Fix.

## Scribbled out (1.6.5)

A pen or marker stroke scribbled over writing rubs out what it lies over and is not kept (Croix: "Scribble out to erase", then "I've experienced other apps that claim scribble to erase works and it sucks…"). The failure he meant is the false one — writing that disappears — so every rule below was held to: real writing never rubs anything out.

- **Where** (`src/pad.js`): `_finish`, for a pen or marker stroke that was not held into a shape, with `o.scribble !== false` (the `scribble="false"` attribute), not on a `draw="answers"` layer → `_scribbleOut(el, info)`. The units under it: each pen and marker stroke (a symbol's strokes under one `group`, from the last reading), each shape by its outline (`shapeOutline`; `closed` for rect, square, ellipse, circle, triangles and polygons; number lines and planes `fixed` — lines to what is drawn on them, never taken), and in a Tidy, My hand or Typeset view what that view shows (`plan.items[].parts`) in place of the strokes it read. `scribbleErase` → the strokes to take; one step `{erase, scribble: true, hide}` (a shown answer under it is hidden again, as the eraser does); `_fade` (320 ms) draws what went and the scribble going; `nib:scribble {erased, hidden, step, joined}`; `undoScribble(step)` undoes it while it is the last step (the Board's note, Deckhand's toast).
- **In pieces** (`_scribbleJoin`): a scribble the board hears as two or more strokes — the pen off the glass for a moment at a turn, a touch frame that lost a fast finger. The strokes just before this one (at most three), each lifted at most 150 ms before the next went down (1.6.6: by the events' own `timeStamp`s, `evTime` — a pen down 40 ms after it lifted is 40 ms after it even when the page, busy drawing, hears it 250 ms later) and no farther from where the next began than the pen could go in that moment (2 × its speed × the gap, 24–80 screen px), with the same pointer type and the same tool, color and size, and nothing done between (they are the last strokes and their `{add}` steps the last steps), are tried with it as one stroke, the longest chain first. If that rubs something out, the pieces' steps are taken off the history too, so one Undo puts back the board as it was before the scribble began. `_lifted` (a `WeakMap`) keeps each pen and marker stroke's down and up times.
- **Is it a scribble** (`scribbleOf`, by its shape alone): a **zig-zag** — legs back and forth along one of six axes, five or more, each at least half the reach, at least 12 px (`scrLegs`); nearly all its turns sharp (`scrSharp` ≥ 0.7: an 8, an m, a ∞ or a cursive word turns round); its legs straight (`scrBent` ≤ 1.3 on the leg a little smoothed: a 3 or an S gone over again is curves, a finger's jitter is not); moving on as it goes (`scrRepeats` < 0.4: a Z, an N or a W gone over again comes back over its own legs); close-packed (L² / hull area ≥ 20, five legs loosely ≥ 25: an M or a W written over a symbol is not); filling what it spans (`scrFill` ≥ 0.6); and not a stroke gone over again (`scrOverlap` at 1.5 px ≥ 0.9 with fill under 0.67: a letter darkened runs along itself all the way and fills little) — or a **coil**: going round one way two whole turns or more (`scrLoopy`, walked in steps of at least 3 px so a hand's jitter is not turning), crossing itself five times or more, packed (≥ 45) and filling (≥ 0.7) what it spans. A coil and any other scribble that goes round and round is `loopy`.
- **What it takes** (`scribbleErase`): a stroke at least 60% inside the scribble and within its band (from the spread of its passes, `band`), and inside it — most of it inside the hull and some way in, or deep in (`SCR`) — goes; a symbol most of which is under it goes whole (its dot and small parts with it). A loopy scribble takes only what it goes over: a stroke its loops cross, pass along away from its ends, or go into the middle of (`into`); one that passes over nothing is a ring around something (`why: 'round it'`) and takes nothing. **Falls short** (1.6.5): when nothing is taken that way, a clear scribble (six legs or more, or a coil) takes the symbols it is on — each a third within its band and two fifths inside it, crossed and gone into, most of the scribble over them — if nothing else is under it to speak of, and never a loop it lies inside (a bubble filled in) or a line it is bounded by. An earlier scribble under it (one that fell short and stayed as ink) goes only with writing it takes.
- **What it never takes**: the **lines it is bounded by** — a long straight line its turns line up along (a fraction bar's top and bottom shaded with the turns a little past them; not a bar it was scribbled along, whose ends it turns at); **the lines of a drawing** — a straight mark joined to a long line it does not cover (a tick, a divider), or a mark a long line runs through (the dot on a number line); **shading** — turns on lines on both sides (or on one drawn straight line, one side of a region), or lying inside a closed shape it mostly fills, with only a little writing inside (a label in a shaded cell): it rubs out nothing and stays as ink. A highlighter never scribbles out.
- **Testing**: `test/scribble_check.js` (node, the engine from `dist/nib.js`; `FULL=1` for every data set) — real writing from the board, the school set and CROHME replayed stroke by stroke, each stroke tried as a scribble over everything written before it: nothing rubbed out (26,629 strokes, 0.03% shaped like a scribble); scribbles of thirteen kinds (zig-zags of every slant and speed, back and forth, rounded, a finger on a touch frame, coils, blots, tangles) over one symbol, a few, and a whole line: 93.7% take what is under them and nothing beside it, 0.45% take something sticking out of them, one symbol scribbled out rubs out nothing 5.6% of the time (the first draft of these rules: 8.1%), with a finger 10.2% (20.8%); 235 fills of shading and drawings rub out nothing; 3,798 marks that are not scribbles (cancel, X, strike-throughs, rings, boxes, underlines, arrows, checks, hatching, stars, spirals, letters and digits written over, strokes gone over again) rub out nothing. `test/scribble_browser.js` in a browser: the pad, and the Board's note and switch.
- **Known limits**: four legs or fewer is ink; a loose five-leg zig-zag (an M or a W over a symbol) is ink; a scribble inside a loop it does not reach the edges of (filling an o or a 0) is filling it, not scribbling it out — go over its edges; a tangle that falls well short of what it is on is ink about one time in four.

## Pipeline

1. **Strokes in.** Arrays of `[x, y]` (or `{x, y}`, or `{points}`), y down, any units. Consecutive duplicate points dropped. With `smooth` > 0 each stroke is smoothed along its path (Gaussian, sigma = `smooth`·`ref`, ends fixed). It is off by default: with the v2 network, 0.03 lifted finger-written expressions from 80% to 89% exact on the stress set, but the v3 network reads finger ink at 90.6% unsmoothed and smoothing only takes a little off that (89.3%).
2. **Scale.** `ref` = median over strokes of max(width, height). Every threshold below is in units of `ref`. The classifier sees a size reference that is held steady while the same ink keeps growing (changes only when the writing's scale moves more than ~15%), so earlier symbols are not re-classified after each stroke.
3. **Segmentation.** Dynamic program over time-ordered strokes (a symbol is 1–4 consecutive strokes whose boxes stay within 1.2·`ref`; only 0.5% of CROHME symbols break time order). A group is scored by the network's not-junk probability (weight `wJunk`; the network has a `junk` class trained on stroke groups that span two symbols), its best class probability (`wClass`), the stroke-join model (P(stroke k and k+1 belong together) from 16 geometric features, weight `wJoin`) and a per-symbol cost (`beta`). Boundaries the join model is very sure of are not crossed (`prune`). Two rules: a tiny mark at the baseline corner of a digit or letter is its own decimal point (`dotPen`), and two tall arcs after an open bracket are `)(` not `x` (`parenPen`). The DP keeps label states, so an optional writing-order context term (`lmSeg`) can score labels while segmenting; it did not help in tuning and is off. **Late strokes** (`delayed`): the DP keeps time order, so a stroke added to an earlier symbol much later (crossing a t at the end, the bar of a + or the second half of an x after the next number) would read as a symbol of its own. Before segmenting, a stroke that touches or crosses ink written two or more strokes earlier (within `lateTol`·`ref`), but neither the stroke just before it nor the one after it, is moved to just after the latest stroke it touches, so the DP sees the symbol's strokes together. After segmenting, two symbols that are not neighbors in time are still merged when their boxes overlap and the network reads the union as one confident symbol that scores `delayGain` better than the two apart; a dot joins only a symbol it sits just above or below (the dot of an i, the dots of ÷), never one it sits beside (a decimal point stays a decimal point). On the stress set where one stroke of a symbol is written last, this takes exact readings from 31% (1.0) to 85.5%.
4. **Classifier.** Each group is seen two ways. (a) Rendered to 4 orientation channels (0/45/90/135°, undirected, so stroke order and direction do not matter) on a 32×32 grid → 5 conv layers (24-48-48-96-96) with 3 max-pools. (b) Its pen path resampled to 48 points (x, y, direction, new-stroke flag) → three 1-D conv layers (32-64-64), max- and mean-pooled. Both, plus 5 scalars (aspect, size relative to `ref`, stroke count, ink length), feed FC 192 → 104 classes (101 CROHME symbols + `%` + `$` + `junk`). Int8 weights with per-channel scales, dequantised at load. `rasterize()` / `onlineFeatures()` in `core.js` mirror `training/raster.py` / `training/online.py` (parity: max pixel difference 1/255; probabilities within 0.016 of the float model; top-1 agreement 299/300).
4b. **A 1 with a long, flat top is a 7** (`flagSeven`, core.js, on the network's output before the writer's memory; 1.6.3). One stroke the network calls a 1 or a 7 (1 at least as likely, the two together at least half): from its start to its highest point is its top; when that is at least 0.6 of the symbol's height across and rises at under ~27° (drop ≤ 0.5 × width), with no foot along the bottom (the stroke after the top running ≤ 0.25 × height sideways once it reaches the bottom 15%), the 1's share goes to the 7 (×0.1 / +0.9). That is how many Europeans write a 1, the writing CROHME has, and a 7 to an American reader — Croix, of a composed test line "12 + 30 =" whose 1 had such a top: "72+30 = 102", then "I would never make the top that long on a one." A writer's own corrections still win (the memory blends in after it). On the CROHME-written sets it reads 7 of the composed set's 369 one-stroke 1s and 22 of the 2019 test's 839 as 7s, so those figures dropped about half a point (below); no 7 in those sets is written that way. `test/check.js` §6b.
5. **School prior.** The network learned CROHME's symbol frequencies (university math: many letters). With `symbols: 'school'` each class probability is re-weighted by (frequency in a middle-school corpus / frequency in CROHME)^`prior` (table `DATA.prior.school`, built by `training/prior.py`; ratios run from 0.2 for rare-in-school symbols to 5.2 for digits).
6. **Symbol fix-ups.** Two stacked dots → `:`; `-` over `-` → `=`; `+` over `-` → `±` (only when nothing sits directly above or below the pair, so fraction bars are safe).
7. **Layout.** In order:
   - square roots, largest first: body = units right of the check mark's lowest point and under the overline (grows when a nested root is found; fraction bars under it and units starting below it are excluded); index = a small unit up and to the left;
   - fraction bars, widest first: a `-` with units clearly above and below it within reach (bar line fitted through the stroke, so sloped bars work), numerator/denominator grown by stacking and by running past the bar ends; operators at the very ends of the bar and units that straddle it are excluded; rejected unless both sides are roughly centered on the bar;
   - lines: units grouped into horizontal bands; a band of up to four small units, each sitting just above and right of a symbol in the line below (or right after the previous one), is merged back as exponents — anything else is a new line (so a short answer line like `88` stays its own line);
   - absolute value: two tall straight strokes read as `1`/brackets are paired into `| |` when both hang below the line of characters and the classifier finds them bar-like (`barH`, `barW`), or when two `1`s stand exactly where bars would: around a plain operand (digits or letters, no relation inside), each at an operand boundary (after an operator, a relation, an open bracket or the start; before one or the end);
   - baselines: units sorted by center x; each unit is Right / Sup / Sub of the current baseline unit by the relation model (MLP over box geometry, typography class and digit/letter flags) with hard rules: operators and open brackets take no scripts; relations are never scripts; a leading minus joins the exponent it starts; a digit after a digit is an exponent only when clearly raised; a cross between two numbers is a times sign and takes no subscript.
8. **Slope.** After the first layout, the writing's slope is measured from the symbols just read, in writing order: neighboring digits and letters of a similar size for their kind (an x-height letter counts as 0.62 of a digit) give the line through their bottoms, signs give the line through their middles; pairs steeper than 17° are dropped (exponents, the next line), the rest vote with weight growing with distance, and at least two must agree. When the agreed slope is more than `deskew` degrees, the same symbols are sheared level and laid out again (they are not re-read; boxes are reported around the ink as written) and `result.skew` says by how much. On the stress sets this lifts drifting lines from 84% to 89% and turned lines from 85% to 87% (v2 network), multi-line work most of all, while straight writing reads exactly as before (the composed set and the real 2013 set unchanged).
9. **Personal memory.** `Pad.relabel` (the "did you mean" menu) stores the corrected symbol's 192-number embedding with its label in localStorage (`nib-memory-v1`, 400 max). A later symbol whose embedding is nearly identical (cosine 0.88 → 0.96 ramps the pull from 0 to 0.8) has its probabilities pulled toward the remembered label. Ink itself is not stored. `Nib.memory.clear()` forgets. Calibrated with `test/memcal.js` on the 2013 writers: corrections fixed 170 later mistakes and disturbed 49 correct readings (5,627 symbols).
10. **Context model.** Viterbi over each row's symbol candidates with a bigram model (CROHME rows + 30,000 synthetic middle-school expressions, weight `lm`), then a final layout pass if any label changed.
11. **Post-layout.** A dot on the baseline is a decimal point, a raised dot is `\cdot`; a small raised circle after a digit or `)` is a degree sign (`degrees`); `x` between two numbers is `\times`.
12. **Output.** LaTeX (KaTeX/MathJax-ready), plain text, per-line tokens, the tree, each symbol with box and candidates, a confidence.

The pad reads in time slices: `Recognizer.warmup()` classifies the stroke groups the DP will need one at a time (newest ink first), then rehearses the whole reading against the cache — the DP and the late-stroke merges, with any group not yet classified noted and read as junk for the moment — classifies what the rehearsal asked for, and repeats until a rehearsal needs nothing new (a late-stroke merge round depends on the answers of the one before). The pad runs it ~14 ms per slice between frames, so the final `recognize()` only reads the cache (checked by `test/check.js`: nothing classified after a warm-up, and the same reading as a cold recognizer); new ink cancels a read in progress. `Nib.recognizeAsync()` does the same for whole expressions. On a board (`separate`), strokes are grouped into expressions first (neighbors on a line: within 1.5 `ref` across and 0.6 up or down; stacked: within 1.6 down and 0.3 across), each expression is read by its own recognizer and kept while its strokes and corrections do not change, and the readings are joined (`\begin{gathered}`, one line per expression).

## In-place views (`src/neat.js`)

A view never changes the reading; it lays the reading out again where it was written.

1. **Units.** `planOf(result, strokes, provider)` works in em of the writer's size: em = the median height of the digits and capitals / 0.7 (small letters count as 0.48 em, `g p q y` as 0.71). Cap height 0.7, x-height 0.48, descender 0.23, math axis 0.25 em (`NEAT`).
2. **Level.** The slope the recognizer measured (`result.skew`) is taken out the same way (shear about the ink's mean x), and each line's baseline is the median bottom of its sized symbols.
3. **Layout**, TeX-like, per line: spacing by atom kind (thin/medium/thick: 1/6, 2/9, 5/18 em) or, where the writer left their own gaps between digits and neighbors, those gaps (`gapsOf`); exponents raised at 0.65 size; fractions centered on the axis at 0.85 size; roots that cover their body (index at 0.6); brackets as tall as what they hold. A gap wider than 1.6 em splits a line into parts that each start where they were written; each part keeps its written width (`fitRow`: the gaps stretch from 0.5 to 2.5 times, typeset glyphs may shrink to 0.8). Relations written one under another on several lines are aligned to their median position.
4. **Providers** give each symbol a glyph in em: **tidy** (`tidyProvider`) fits the writer's own strokes to the symbol's kind (height, width or larger side; sitting on the baseline, hanging, or centered on the axis — `sizeSpec`), smooths the wobble, makes nearly straight strokes straight and nearly level or upright ones exact (`tidyPaths`), squares signs (`squareSign`: `=` bars level, equal and stacked; `+` upright through its bar's middle; `÷` dots over the middle) and keeps a root's tick while redrawing its overline to cover the body (`tidyRadical`). Point counts are kept so ink can morph into it. **hand** (`handProvider`) uses the Replicator's strokes for the character (box space → em as the font does: 1.1 em wide, baseline 0.95 em below the top of a 1.25 em box), with tidy stand-ins for characters the writer never wrote. **typeset** (`typeProvider`) uses KaTeX's metrics and fonts (`DATA.tsfm`, `DATA.tsfont`: Main, Math italic for letters, Size1; registered at runtime as "Nib KaTeX …" with the system's math fonts as fallback), rules for fraction bars, a drawn radical, `≠` as `=` with a stroke through it.
5. **Items.** `drawablesOf(plan)` turns the plan into items `{key, src (stroke indices), label, box, parts}` in ink coordinates with parts `ink` (paths), `text`, `rule` and `line`; `drawItems(ctx, items)` draws them. `Nib.neaten` / `Nib.typeset` return these for use outside a pad.
6. **In a pad.** After each reading the pad builds a plan per expression and ties every item to its stroke objects (`from`), so the eraser takes away what it touches as shown, undo restores it, and items survive edits elsewhere. Switching views (or a new hand) re-plans without re-reading; items morph point by point when the stroke counts match and otherwise cross-fade between boxes (260 ms, off with `motion="false"` or reduced motion).
7. **The saved hand.** `Nib.hand.load()` keeps a Replicator file in localStorage (`nib-hand-v1`, dated). `handLoad()` also looks at the Replicator's own saved work (`replicator-v1`, dated since 1.2): the newer of the two wins, so letters redone in the Replicator are never hidden by an older file. Both offline pages opened from one computer in Chrome share that storage (the storage event updates an open board); published pages each have their own, so there the file is the way across.

## Answers after "=" (`src/solve.js`, drawn by `src/neat.js`)

1. **Tokens** (`tokensOf`): the reading tree becomes numbers (digits and one point; a comma before exactly three digits is a thousands comma), operators (× and ⋅ are times, ÷ and / divide), relations, brackets, absolute-value bars, letters (with subscripts), π, fractions, roots, superscripts, % and !; a bar (`over` node, LaTeX `\overline`) over digits straight after a number with a point makes it a repeating decimal (`rep` on the number token; `qOfRepeating`: 0.1 with 6 repeating is (16 − 1)/90), and a bar over anything else means no answer. The handwriting reader makes no `over` nodes, so this is LaTeX only for now. Anything else (∑, sin, $, ±, ∞) means no answer, and so does a run of letters that spells a unit or word (`SOLVE_WORDS`: cm, ft, GCF, …). Classroom readings: where × can be a times sign (`times` not false), an x between a number, %, closing bracket or bar, fraction, power, root or ! and a number or fraction is a times sign ("3 x 4", "2/3 x 6"); "of" after one of those is times ("15% of 80"), and so is "0f" (an o read as a zero) after anything but a number.
2. **Parse** (`solveParse`): + and − over ×, ÷ and implicit products, left to right (6 ÷ 2(1+2) = 9), except that a slash between two whole numbers is a fraction (2/3x = ⅔x) and a term with letters after ÷ or / is divided by as a whole (6x ÷ 3x = 2, C ÷ 2π); a lone letter just before a bracket of numbers is function notation (f(2)) and is refused; unary minus below powers (−2² = −4), powers right-associative, then ! and %. A whole number followed by a fraction of whole numbers is a mixed number; any other number or fraction straight after something without an operation (x4, (2)3, 15%80, ½ 0) is refused.
3. **Evaluate** (`solveEval`): coefficients are exact fractions `{n, d}` (BigInt, at most 4096 bits) or floats `{f}` (π, roots that do not come out, fractional powers of non-perfect powers); values are polynomials `Map(monomial → coefficient)` (at most 64 terms, degree 24), so letters are combined and multiplied out, and a polynomial divides by a single term when every term divides (6x² ÷ 3x = 2x). A float result below a billion within 1e-13 (relative) of a whole number, a fraction with denominator ≤ 1000 or a decimal of six significant digits becomes exact (√2·√2 = 2, π·7² ÷ (π·50²) = 0.0196), never zero; π and roots that do not come out are made raw and never snap. A float sum that cancels to within 1e-10 of its parts is refused (π − 3.14159265358979: zero or not cannot be told), and so is one that comes out exactly zero from an exact number and an approximation (π − 3.141592653589793); two approximations that cancel exactly (π − π) give 0. Dividing by letters, powers with letters in them, roots or bars around letters: no answer. `info` records how the question was asked (decimals, %, fractions stacked or with a slash, mixed numbers, ÷, negative powers, π, a given value put in).
4. **Form**: a lone number turns into its other form (decimal → fraction or mixed number; proper fraction → decimal; improper fraction in lowest terms → mixed number; unreduced fraction → reduced; mixed number → improper fraction; % → decimal; a lone whole number gets nothing; a fraction with a slash counts as a fraction, a sign inside one as a sign on it). Percents: a sum of percents, or anything × 100%, answers as a percent; a percent added to anything else is refused. Otherwise: approximate → three decimal places (three significant digits below 1) cut, never rounded, and an ellipsis; decimals or % in the question → a decimal (exact up to 12 places; repeating with a bar when the repeat is at most 6 digits after at most 6; else four places cut and an ellipsis); fractions → a fraction, or a mixed number when the question had one; ÷ of whole numbers → a decimal; else a fraction. Letters: standard form (degree, then alphabetical exponents: a² − 2ab + b²); no answer when the expression is already a sum of distinct simplest terms, or one such sum over a whole number with nothing to cancel ((x + 1)/2), unless signs are doubled (x + −3) or a given value goes in (`simplified`).
5. **Lines** (`solveRows`): a line whose last symbol is "=" (and no other relation) is answered, using what lies between it and the "=" before it; every earlier part of a chain must be worked out and agree with it ("2(3 + 4) = 2 · 7 =" → 14; "3 + 4 = 7 + 1 =", "1 ÷ 0 = 2 + 3 =" and "3 + 4 = x + 2x =" → nothing), except that letters before numbers stand for them ("A = lw = 3 · 4 =" → 12). A line "x = …" whose right side works out to a number gives x for later lines of the same expression on a board (each cluster has its own letters, so two problems on one board never share an x). When it ends the working of an equation in x alone on the line just above, the value is soft: it is put in only when there is nothing else to do (x + 1 = → 5, 3x + 2x = → 5x); a value under a formula with several letters (d = rt, then r = 60) is given outright. "x = x + 1" is an equation, not a value, and an equation that the given values do not satisfy makes its letters unknowns again.
6. **Equations** (`solveFor`): every line with one relation (=, <, >, ≤, ≥) and two sides is kept with its two sides' trees; a line with a relation and letters that cannot be kept (|x| = 5, √x, 2ˣ, a chain, a compound inequality, a percent added to a number, a misread line) is kept as a block. A line that is just a letter and a relation ("x =", "x <") takes the nearest line above with that letter that answers its kind of question (a solved "x = 4" check under an inequality is passed over; a block, or any other line in the way, means no answer). Each side is worked out as a fraction of polynomials (`ratEval`), so a letter may sit underneath (3/x = 6/10), with the values given above put in for every letter but the asked one (A = lw, l = 5, w = 3: A = 15); P = left·right-denominator − right·left-denominator. One letter: linear (−b/a), or a·xⁿ + b with n = 2 (±; nothing for a negative; a square root that does not come out is a cut decimal, x² = 50 → ±7.071…) or 3, or a quadratic a·x² + b·x + c with b ≠ 0 and exact coefficients (`quadRoots`: scaled to whole numbers with no common factor and a > 0; D = b² − 4ac; D < 0 no answer; D = 0 one root; D a perfect square two rational roots, smallest first; otherwise (p ± k√m)/d with m square-free and p, k, d sharing no factor, carried as `rad` with float approximations in `vals` for comparing; D over 10¹⁰ is refused); a value that makes a denominator zero is no answer, for any root (`settle`). Answers with two roots are written `-3,\;-2` (kind `roots`, a `,` node between them) and radicals `\frac{-3\pm\sqrt{5}}{2}` or `-2\pm\sqrt{2}` (kind `radical`, a `root` node; `rootsAnswer`); their `value` is null. Working lines are compared as sets of roots (`sameSettle` sorts them). Several letters (at most 3, linear, no letter underneath): all the equations on the lines right next to it in the same piece of writing, solved exactly by elimination (`systemFor`) — if they contradict each other there is no answer, and a letter is answered when they pin it down even if others stay free (y = 0 under two equations in x, y, z); where both the given values and the system speak, they must agree (so a wrong "x = 5" under x + y = 10 and x − y = 2 gets no y). **Rearranging** (`rearrangedFor`, `rearrange`): when neither settles the letter, an equation in several letters (values given above put in first) is solved for it if it appears only to the first power and never in a denominator: P = A·v + B gives v = −B/A, written as a polynomial when A is a number (2x + 3y = 12 → −2/3 x + 4; P = 2l + 2w → 1/2 P − w: the canonical form is terms, with a fraction coefficient, like every other expression answer), divided out when A is one term that divides every term of B (xy + xz = x → −z + 1), else one fraction scaled to whole-number coefficients with no common factor and a positive first term underneath (A = lw → A/l, y = mx + b → (y − b)/m, A = ½bh → 2A/b). Terms are in standard order except that a plus term goes first among those of the highest degree (y − b, not −b + y). No answer when the line is already "v = …" with no v on the right (y = 2x + 1, then y =), when B is 0 and A has letters (xy = 0: y = 0 only if x ≠ 0), when a bottom of several terms shares a letter with the top (it might cancel: (a + b)y = a² − b²), with π or another approximation in it, or for an inequality. The lines right next to it in the same piece of writing must each rearrange to the same thing (its working: 2x + 3y = 12, 3y = −2x + 12); a line beside it with its letters but not v, or one that rearranges differently, means it may be a system, and there is no answer. The answer is kind `expression`. The value's form: `styleOf` for a value given outright ("x = 10 ÷ 4" → 2.5), otherwise decimals in the equation give a decimal, mixed numbers a mixed number, anything else a fraction (`eqStyleOf`). The working right above a one-letter line must settle to the same thing line by line (a slip in a step gets no answer), up to a solved line ("x = 4") that ends an earlier problem. Inequalities: one letter, linear, nothing underneath; the relation flips with a negative coefficient, and the number is given only when the relation written after the letter is the right one. ± answers have kind `plusminus`. On a board, an "x =" whose piece of writing has no line with x may use the equations of the piece just above it (within four writing heights, overlapping across).
7. **Drawing**: answers are reading-tree nodes (digits, '.', '-', ±, %, letters with powers, fractions, `over` for the repeating bar, three points for the ellipsis) laid out by `placeAnswers` after the "=" (or <, >, ≤, ≥) item on the line's baseline at the writer's em, with TeX's space after a relation plus the writer's own gap. Glyphs come from `sampleBook` (8 Nov 2026: it used to take the clearest instance anywhere on the board, and on Croix's tablet a lone stroke read as a 1 at high certainty — a stroke of an X, or a letter of "What is the value of c?" — drew √244's answer as "\5.620…"): an instance read surely (p ≥ 0.6) from the answer's own part first (`book.near(k)`, the k-th of `res.parts`; the pad passes each part's), then from the board's equations (parts with =, <, >, ≤, ≥ or ≠; words on the board never lend their letters-read-as-digits), the most typical of them (the least summed distance in shape — 24 points along the strokes, centered and scaled by the larger side, either direction — to the others and to the reference glyph), not the surest; else the Replicator hand; else for a letter any instance of it on the board; else a built-in hand (`DEFAULT_HAND`: Catmull-Rom control points for 0–9 . − + ± , and a–z, A–Z); else any instance of it. `joinParts` tags each symbol with its `part`. Roots in answers (`root` nodes) are drawn by the view's radical (`synthRadical` in tidy when there is no ink to take it from, the hand's √, or the typeset one). In the Ink view only the answers are laid out, after the raw "=", and sheared back onto the line's slope (`unlevel`). Points in tidy views are drawn as dots of 0.042 em so a decimal point is never lost.
8. **x as a letter** (`times: false`, the Board's setting): `allowedMask` drops × from the symbol set and `foldTimes` gives the network's × score to x (so a cross written like × is an x, never a +), and the solver never turns an x into a times sign; multiplication is a raised dot or brackets. Recognition helps both: a dot halfway up its neighbors is heard by the context model as a times dot (it would otherwise rather hear x − x than x . x), a tiny mark beside a digit or letter (baseline or middle) is never part of it (121·21 is not 12121), and a ")" with no "(" (or a "(" never closed) takes the bracket from the 1 most likely to be one (`balanceBrackets`).
9. **Misreadings that must not become answers**: two symbols one above the other read as neighbors on a line (a ½ whose bar was missed, read as −12), or a power or index straight above or below its base, are marked `doubt` by `markStacked` and get no answer; so does a plus sign straight after another sign (5 + + 2x). A dot level with a fraction bar between fractions is a times dot. On a board, writing that carries on past a gap on the same line after a sign (the left piece ends with + − × ÷ ·, or the right one begins with + × ÷ · =, within six writing heights) is worked out as one problem ("13.999 +   2 + 7 =" → 22.999); two problems on one line stay two.
10. **In the pad**: `solve` is a view option (no re-read). Answer items carry no strokes (`from: []`) but the "=" strokes they follow (`eqFrom`); they are drawn in `--nib-answer`, glide between views by id, fade out when they go, vanish at once when their "=" is erased, and step aside the moment new ink (even a stroke in progress) reaches their box. With `reveal` they are laid out the same way but drawn out of focus, where they go, until tapped (1.6.3; until then one gold "?" chip per answer): their length shows, not what they say. A picture of the board (`exportImage`, `image()`) leaves them out — no one can tap a picture, and a blur is no lock — and an answer key (`answers: 'all'`) draws them sharp. `pad.answers` lists `{line, latex, text, kind, exact, value}`; `solvedLatex()` writes them in.
11. **An answer keeps its look** (1.6.5): an answer's symbols come from `sampleBook` — the problem's own, the board's equations' most typical, the Replicator hand, the built-in glyphs — and the book changes as the board fills, so every replan used to redraw every answer with the latest choice (Croix: "I do not like when you retroactively change the font in answers you already put up"). `_replan` now gives `inPlace` a `sampleFor(row)`: per answer — the sids of its line's strokes, its LaTeX, the view and whether a Replicator hand is set (`_looks`, a `Map`) — each symbol is taken from the book once (a copy of its points, `src` the board strokes it came from: `book.srcOf`, `dflt` for a built-in glyph) and kept. A changed line or answer, or another view, is a new key and is drawn from the book as it is then; looks not on the board are let go past 400. `getState` saves them as `looks: [{strokes: [line's stroke indexes], answer, view, hand, g: {label: {s: [stroke indexes]} | {d: 1} | {p: [storePack strings]}}}]` and `setState` puts them back (the Board's `okPage` / `packPage` carry them, checked by `okLook`), so a reload or a page switch draws each answer as it was. `test/looks_browser.js`.

## Replicator (`replicator/core.js`, `replicator/template.html`)

- **Outlines.** Each stroke is resampled every 6 font units; hooks go first (`dehook`: a turn of more than 130° within 12 units of an end at smoothing 1–2, 20 units from 3 up, and never more than an eighth of the stroke: a pen landing or lifting, while the flag of a 1, about three times as long, stays); then a Gaussian along the path (`SMOOTH_SIGMA`, σ 0–78 units for strengths 0–10; default 5, and files saved before strengths went to 10 move up by 3) applied piece by piece between corners (a turn over 1.2 rad stays sharp), the ends held; then the stroke is swept with a round pen (`weight`) and traced. **Even out letter sizes** (`fitToGuides`) scales a character towards the height its kind should have (cap, x-height; descenders hang from the x-height line) within 0.7–1.4.
- **Sentences.** The ruled lines are boxes laid end to end (line units: the box height; `toLine`/`toBox`). `alignSentence(lines, text, templates)` matches the strokes to the text by dynamic programming over (strokes used, characters written): a character is 1–4 consecutive strokes on one line, scored by a greedy point-cloud distance (`cloud`/`cloudDist`, the $P recognizer's, 24 points: stroke order and direction do not matter) to its box drawing and its last three learned samples; a character can be left out or a stroke be stray at a fixed cost; the late strokes of i, j and t (small marks written back to the left, after the word) are set aside and given afterwards to the letter whose ink they sit over; a beam (1.6) keeps it to about 0.3 s for a long sentence. A letter is learned only if its distance is under `accept` (0.085) and clearly nearer its own character than any other (0.8 of the nearest rival); a sentence teaches nothing at all if under 75% of its letters got strokes, or — only when it does not line up word for word — the mean distance is over 1.3 × accept, or under 35% of the letters that have a box drawing were learned (8 Nov 2026: Croix's sentence on his tablet lined up letter for letter and was turned away whole; now `lined` — every text word on its own run of strokes, apart from the next on the same line by more than any gap inside either, at most one word with no strokes — keeps each letter's own verdict; a wrong sentence crossed a word break in 48 of 48 tries; `alignSentence` also returns `lined` and `words` [{text, missing, extra, cross}]) (the wrong sentence: 0 letters over 48 tries; sentences written far less like the boxes are still learned from). `averageGlyph` takes the box drawing and up to 12 samples per character, keeps those with the commonest stroke count, drops outliers (over 2.2 × the median distance to the medoid), matches strokes by order and direction, centers them on the medoid and averages 40 points per stroke. The page keeps samples per character in its saved work (and the `.json`), and the averages beside the box drawings as `avg`, which Nib's `handOf` uses for My hand.
- **The eraser** (8 Nov 2026, Croix: "There was also no eraser besides clearing the whole thing."): the Eraser button or E (off while typing), or a pen whose `buttons & 32` / `button 5` says its eraser end is down, makes a stroke a rub (`pointer()` passes `rub` to each callback); a stroke the rub passes within 0.07 box (0.09 line) of — segment to segment, crossings included — goes, as one Undo step (`history` {ch, erased: [{i, s}]} for a box; `strip.hist` {add} / {erased} for the lines, Clear included). Rubbing the lines after a Learn takes that Learn back first (`unlearn`), leaving the writing to fix.

## Training data and models

- **CROHME** (online handwritten math expressions, InkML with symbol-level segmentation and MathML ground truth). CROHME 2019 from the GitHub mirror `Mahdiyeh-Asgharpour/CROHME2019` (train 8,901; CROHME 2014 test 986; CROHME 2019 test 1,199) and the CROHME 2012 and 2013 test sets from `ThomasLech/CROHME_extractor` (488 + 671, not part of the 2019 release). Splits: **train** = 2019 train + 2014 test + 2012 test (10,375 expressions, ~102k symbols); **tuning** = 2013 test (671) and `synth_t13` (700 school expressions composed from the 2013 writers' symbols); **reported test** = 2019 test (1,199) and `test/school_synth.jsonl` (700 composed from the 2019 test writers), never used for training or tuning. CROHME is distributed for research use; the models here are derived from it.
- Classifier v2: 14 epochs over six augmented renderings + one clean, AdamW, cosine schedule, label smoothing, class weights; 90.4% symbol accuracy (99.0% top-3) on the 2013 writers over all 101 classes. v1 (a smaller raster-only CNN on less data) reached 59.7% / 75.7% on the two tuning sets where v2 reaches 61.8% / 80.6% before tuning.
- Classifier v3 (shipped in 1.1): v2 fine-tuned for 6 more epochs (`training/train_v3.py`), half of them rendered with `training/aug3.py` on top of v2's augmentation: strokes drawn backwards (30%), the strokes of a symbol in another order (35%), mouse-like sparse pixel-snapped points (20%) and finger-like wobble (20%). On the 2013 writers' symbols it keeps v2's accuracy on clean ink (90.4%) and reads the same symbols rewritten messily far better (87.4% vs 82.0%). End to end it raised every stress set (finger 80% → 91%), the composed 2019-writer set (90.2% → 91.5% on the stress tool's sample) and the real 2019 school set, and is slightly lower on the 2013 tuning sets (real 63.4% → 60.7%, composed 89.1% → 88.6%, mostly single letters of ambiguous case: s/S, v/V, w/u). The epoch-4 model and an average of epochs 4–6 were both weaker overall. Remembered corrections from 1.0 do not carry over (the memory is keyed to the network).
- Classifier v4s50 (shipped in 1.5.3; `training/README.md` §2c): v3 fine-tuned on Croix's own drill writing (47 checked expressions from four training files, every 5th drill item held out), then blended halfway back to v3 in weight space (`training/soup_v4.py`, WiSE-FT). The fine-tune alone read his hand better but cost the school set 2 expressions (× read as x: every cross he writes is the letter); the blend keeps its gains on his held-out items (11/13 → 12/13 exact, symbols 90 → 91 of 93) with the school set unchanged (384/546), the composed set unchanged (90.6%), the full 2019 set 663 → 660 of 1187 (3 of the 9 new misses are × read as x, which the Board never reads) and int8 parity 299/300. **In later rounds keep the held-out items (drill item % 5 == 0) out of training**, and gate on them next to §4's sets. A new network starts with an empty correction memory (the memory holds its embeddings); the Board teaches it again, once, from the corrected symbols in the training log (`relearn` in `board/board.js`, `Nib.memory.model`).
- Join and relation MLPs (scikit-learn): 95.3% and 97.1% on the 2013 writers.
- **Training log** (the Board's "Keep writing for training", `train` on a pad). A piece of writing (one part of a board, as `clustersOf` groups it) is kept once it has been read and left alone for 4 s — no stroke added to it or near it, nothing erased in it — or when the page it is on is left (another page, Clear, the page deleted, `pagehide`); a Fix keeps its piece again at once. Its key is its strokes' ids, a hash of each stroke's points at 0.1 px (the precision pages are saved at), so the same writing has the same key after a reload or on a page taken out again, and keeping a key again replaces its record. A record: `{key, t (ms since 1970), w, h (the board in CSS px), view, strokes: [[[x, y, t], ...]] (from the piece's top-left, 0.1 px, t in ms from its first point; 0 for strokes put back from storage), types: ['pen' | 'touch' | 'mouse' | null per stroke], widths (1.5.2: the largest contact width the browser gave each stroke, or null), latex (the reading as shown, corrections in), symbols: [{label, strokes: [indices into strokes]}], fixed: [{strokes: [indices], label}] (the writer's corrections), answered (an answer was worked out for one of its lines, shown or waiting for a tap), confidence}`; at most 3,000 records and about 4 MB, the oldest dropped first. A saved file is `{format: 'nib-training', version: 1, exported (ISO date), engine (Nib's version), records}`. **To fine-tune from it:** (1) `python3 -I tools/training_to_jsonl.py Nib-training-*.json training/scratch/data/board.jsonl --repeat-fixed 3` (rows `{id, writer: 'board', truth, strokes: [[x, y], ...] (y down, like CROHME), symbols, orphans, latex, source: 'board', types, fixed, split: 'board'}`; a times dot becomes `.`, `:` two dots, a degree sign `o`, and a label the classifier has no class for is left out; the same record in several files is taken once; fixed rows written 3 times weigh the corrections more); (2) add `'board'` to the train sources in `training/build_samples_v2.py` (`('train', ('train', 'valid', 't12', 'board'))`) and run it; (3) re-render the epochs (`make_epochs_v2.py 6 2`, `make_epochs_v3.py 4 2`; both skip files that exist, so move the old `ep2/` and `ep3/` `.npz` away first); (4) run the fine-tune script, `python3 train_v3.py scratch/work/model_v4.npz` (6 epochs from the v2 checkpoint; give it a new name, as it resumes from `<out>.ckpt`); (5) `python3 ../build.py scratch/work/model_v4.npz`, `node ../test/parity.js scratch/work/model_v4.npz` and the measurements in `training/README.md` §4, which must not drop. Symbols nobody fixed carry the reader's own guess, so a misreading that was not corrected teaches the mistake: the more corrected the log, the better, and a few hundred expressions is a start, not a retrain. Remembered corrections (`nib-memory-v1`) do not carry over to a new network.
- **Training drill** (the Board, Settings → Training → Start the drill; `board/board.js`, `DRILL`). Free writing only ever has the reader's own guess as its label; the drill gives the whole expression a true label. Drill `v1` is 60 prompts stored as LaTeX with `how` ('any' | 'pen' | 'finger') and an optional `note` ('small', 'large', 'fast', 'your usual 4', 'a closed 4', 'fraction small and a little off-centre'): arithmetic, decimals and %, fractions and mixed numbers, powers, roots and absolute value, like terms and brackets, two- and three-line equations with "x =" under them, inequalities and ≠, brackets for times, the same expression small/large/fast, two kinds of 4, and five for the finger. Starting turns training on, goes to a new page (unless the page is empty) and shows a bar across the top of the surface (an overlay under the rail, on every rail position; the pad keeps its size, so nothing is refitted). The prompt is drawn with KaTeX when `window.katex` exists, otherwise (both Board builds: KaTeX is not in them) as plain text in the typeset fonts (`typeFontsReady`): fractions a/b with "(stacked)", √ and ∛, superscript digits, letters in the math italic. While a prompt shows, `pad.trainMeta = {expect, drill: 'v1', item, how, note?}`. The reading line waits until the board has read everything and the newest stroke ended 1 s ago (with Erase on it says so instead: the pen would rub out, not write); it takes the piece (one part of `clustersOf`) whose lines, joined with ` \\ `, match the prompt, else the newest piece, and shows it with its answers, gold when `normTex(reading) === normTex(expect)` (whitespace, `\left`/`\right` and `\,` spacing dropped; `·` and `⋅` = `\cdot`; `\dfrac` = `\frac`, `\frac12` = `\frac{1}{2}`, `x^2` = `x^{2}`; `\leq` = `\le` and the like; `\%` = `%`; one final `=` optional), coral otherwise. A finger prompt whose piece has a stroke from a pen or a mouse (`strokeInfo`) gets a coral border and says so; it is still kept. **Right**: `trainKeep(piece)`, `trainVerify(key, true)`, then `trainMeta = null` and Clear (anything else on the page is kept as free writing, without the prompt), next prompt. **Fix**: Fix mode; `relabel` keeps the piece again with `fixed`, the reading line follows, and Right keeps it fixed and verified. **Skip**: `trainIgnore()` and Clear, the item noted as skipped. **Stop**: bar hidden, `trainMeta` null, the place kept. Turning training off stops the drill. Progress: `nib-drill-v1` = `{v: 1, drill, pos (0-based; N = finished), skipped: [items], done: [record keys], fixed: [items], active}`, written at each step; `active` brings the bar back after a reload. After the last prompt the bar shows "d done, s skipped, f fixed" (done = Right presses; the skipped items listed) with Save training and Start again. **Records** from the drill carry, besides the usual fields, `expect` (the prompt's LaTeX), `drill`, `item` (1-based), `how`, `note` (when the prompt has one) and, after Right, `verified: true`; records made while a prompt showed but not verified (kept after 4 s, or on a page change) have `expect` but no `verified`. **The converter** copies those fields into each row, adds `gold: true` when `verified` is true and the reading equals `expect` under the same normalization (`norm_tex`, kept in step with `normTex`), `gold: false` otherwise, prints the number of gold rows, and with `--verified-only` writes only verified rows. **Using gold rows in fine-tuning**: a gold row's symbols and structure are what the writer meant, checked by the writer, so it is the one kind of board row that can (a) be trusted for the symbol classifier without the "uncorrected misreading teaches the mistake" caveat above — give gold rows the weight fixed rows get (`--repeat-fixed`, or write them twice), and drop or down-weight non-gold drill rows (`--verified-only` drops the unchecked; a verified row that is not gold means the writer accepted a reading other than the prompt, so treat it like free writing); (b) train the join and relation models and the context model, which need true expression structure; and (c) above all serve as an expression-level held-out set from this writer's real hand (finger and pen apart, by `how` and `types`): keep a fixed share of drill items (for example every fifth item, or one whole run of the drill) out of training and report exact-match on it next to the measurements in `training/README.md` §4, before and after each fine-tune. The `item` lets the same prompt be compared across runs and across pen and finger.

## Tuning knobs (options to `recognize`)

All tuned by grid search on the two tuning sets (mean of the real and composed exact-match rates).

| Option | Default | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `symbols` | `'school'` | allowed classes; `'all'`, `'numbers'`, or an array of labels |
| `prior` | 0.5 | strength of the school prior (only for `symbols: 'school'`; 0 = off) |
| `wJunk`, `wClass`, `wJoin`, `beta` | 2, 1, 0.2, 0.5 | segmentation scoring (see Pipeline 3) |
| `prune` | 0.03 | skip stroke groups across a boundary the join model is this sure about (halves classifier calls, no accuracy change) |
| `dotPen`, `parenPen` | 3, 3 | decimal-point and `)(` rules |
| `lm` | 0.35 | context-model weight (0 = off) |
| `lmSeg` | 0 | writing-order context during segmentation (off) |
| `size` | 0 | height-consistency term in the context model (hurt in tuning, off) |
| `maxGap` | 1.6 | furthest a numerator/denominator may sit from its bar, in `ref` |
| `barH`, `barW` | 1.1, 0.15 | absolute-value bar: height vs. the line's characters, width/height |
| `delayed`, `lateTol`, `delayGain` | true, 0.08, 0.3 | join late strokes back to their symbol (Pipeline 3) |
| `deskew` | 3 | level writing that slopes more than this many degrees (0 = off; Pipeline 8) |
| `skewW`, `skewAgree` | 2.5, 0.5 | how much agreeing evidence the slope needs (weight, share of all votes) |
| `smooth` | 0 | smooth each stroke along its path, sigma in `ref` (the pad uses 0.03 for finger ink) |
| `lines`, `degrees`, `memory` | true | split lines; degree signs; apply remembered corrections |
| `fix` | — | `[{strokes: [indices], label}]` forces a symbol (used by `Pad.relabel`) |

## Results

Measured on the CROHME 2019 test writers, never used for training or tuning (`node test/eval.js test full school`, `node test/eval_synth.js`, `node test/eval.js test full all 100000 '{"prior":0}'`):

| Set | Expressions | Exact | Detail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Middle-school math composed from those writers' real symbols | 700 | 89.7% (1.6.2: 90.6%, 1.3: 89.3%, 1.0: 86.7%; 1.6.3 reads their long-topped 1s as 7s) | token error 1.9%; arithmetic 95, equations 87, inequalities 87, fractions 91, powers 95, roots 96, percents/money 91, ratios 90, formulas 85, absolute value 83, multi-line 84 |
| Real expressions in middle-school notation | 546 | 69.8% (1.6.2: 70.3%, 1.3: 70.0%, 1.0: 69.0%) | structure right 82.6%; symbols segmented right 98.8%, labeled right 95.6% |
| Full CROHME 2019 test, all symbols, no school prior | 1,187 | 55.3% (1.6.2: 55.9%, 1.0: 55.6%) | structure 68.7%; symbols 98.0% / 93.5% |

Messy writing (`node test/stress.js` rewrites the composed set; each variant scored on every third expression, `EVERY=3 SYNTH=training/scratch/data/stress_<variant>.jsonl node test/eval_synth.js`), 1.0 → 1.1: neat 88.5 → 91.5, strokes drawn backwards 83.8 → 90.2, strokes of a symbol reordered 83.3 → 87.6, one stroke added at the end 31.2 → 85.5, mouse 87.6 → 90.2, finger 78.2 → 90.6, line turned 6–12° 82.9 → 88.0, line drifting 6–12° 82.1 → 90.2, two or three at once 62.8 → 83.3. In 1.1 the network (v3) accounts for most of the backwards, reordered, finger and mixed gains, the late-stroke stage for the late strokes, the slope stage for turned and drifting lines (and the multi-line category), and the absolute-value rule for `| |` (46% → 83% on the composed set).

In 1.4 the gains are in arithmetic and powers: a raised dot is no longer heard as a minus by the context model, a dot is no longer swallowed by the digit beside it, brackets back to back (")(") are no longer read as an x when they are as tall as the digits, and an unpaired bracket takes back the "(" or ")" that was read as a 1. On the Board's own convention (x a letter, · and brackets for times: 17 algebra and arithmetic problems × 30 composed writers), 1.3 read 85.9% right and gave a wrong answer 4.9% of the time; 1.4 reads 91.4% right and gives a wrong answer 2.4% of the time (`2x(x+5)` 29/30, `x·x` 17 → 25/30, `(121)(21)` 14 → 23/30). Equations with "x =" under them (12 kinds × 12 writers): 90% answered right, 1.4% wrong.

1.4.1 came from a real board (Croix's screenshot of 4 Nov, replayed with the tools above; the replay gave back the same three wrong answers his board had shown). Three changes, none of which moves any figure above (composed, school and full sets identical to the expression; the convention and equation probes identical): a **lopsided fraction** — one side squarely on the bar, the other written off to one side of it, such as a ½ whose 2 runs out past the end of a short bar, or whose 1 sits high over its left end — is still a fraction (`takeFractions`: the off side is taken if it overlaps 30% of the bar and its middle is within 0.6 bar widths + 0.45 ref of the bar's); a **lone bracket** standing like the digits beside it inside a number, with 1 as a real second guess (≥ 0.15), is the 1 (a curved 1 in 13 + 4 read as "(3 + 4"; not in powers or indices, and not next to a letter); and a **minus wholly above** the symbol after it is doubted, so the line gets no answer (a ½ whose numerator went to the line above would otherwise be read "7 + −2" and answered 5). On the replayed board 12 of its 13 problems are now answered right and none wrong; the thirteenth, 5x + 2x with its first x written as an upright cross, reads 5 + + 2x and gets no answer (both crosses are pluses to the classifier, 0.98 and 0.95).

The full-set figure is in the range published research models report on this benchmark (most of those read images, not ink, and run on servers). On the tuning sets 1.1 reaches 61.3% (real 2013 school-style) and 88.6% (composed from the 2013 writers); 1.0 reached 63.9% and 87.3%, and the first version of the engine (v1 network, untuned) 59.7% and 75.7%.

Speed (headless Chrome on a 2-core server, shared with other work): the 14-stroke sample is read 0.9 s after navigation including page load; a 6-symbol expression is ready 0.75 s after the last pen-up, of which 0.45 s is the deliberate rest delay. In Node a whole 8–20-stroke expression takes 0.35–1.2 s from cold and about 10 ms once its strokes are cached. One stroke group costs about 25 ms to classify (the CNN dominates), so the views and the Board are dominated by reading, not drawing: with the processor slowed 4×, a new expression written beside 12 others (90 strokes) on the Board shows in 3.5–4.5 s, and no task in that time runs longer than about 0.25 s (`test/board_browser.js`).

## Known weak spots

- The views: a part whose newest stroke ended less than `settle` ms ago (the Board: 2,500) is left out of the plan, answers included, and drawn as raw ink; a timer replans when it has rested (`Pad._replan`). Strokes restored from storage carry no time and count as old.
- The first training file (7 Nov, his tablet; `test/fixtures/training_2026-11-07.json`, `test/solve_check.js` §21) brought three reading rules: an **open 4** — an "L" with the upright drawn a little to its right, not touching, which the join model took for two symbols (c 1, L 1, ( 1) — is checked on its shape and joined when the classifier sees a 4 in the pair (`Recognizer.openFour`: the join probability is lifted to 0.75 and the pair's junk share capped at 0.1); a **tiny ring** between digits or letters, under 0.3 of their height, is a dot (`fixRow`), so a times dot drawn as a little loop is no longer a 0; and **three 1s of one height** are 111, not |1| (`fixBars`). Composed set 90.1 → 90.6%, real school set unchanged. Also from his writing: a finger's tap within 30 s of the pen is the heel of the hand and is dropped (`Pad._finish`), and a root's index may sit lower when it is clearly left of the tick.
- The second training file (7 Nov, the drill; `test/fixtures/training_2026-11-07b.json`, §22) explained "my pen stopped reading": on prompt 7 he wrote 2 · 22 + 2 = with the spaces the prompt shows, nearness split it into three pieces (`2\cdot`, `22+`, `2=`: the short = and · strokes pull the median stroke size down), he pressed Fix, undid the =, and from then on the Fix layer swallowed every pen stroke. Now Fix is a pad mode in which writing writes (and leaves Fix), the drill bar says when Erase is on, and pieces ending or beginning in an operator are joined and read again (`joinOperands`). Still open from that file: his **6** — a long slanted stem with a small loop at the foot — reads as 4 (46 → 44), and a **1** next to 7 read as a bracket (17+71 → 17+7)); both wait for the fine-tune on his gold rows rather than a rule.
- The third training file (7 Nov evening, prompts 1–37; `test/fixtures/training_2026-11-07c.json`, §22, 32 gold rows): the two-line prompts never matched because "x =" written well under its equation was a piece of its own (now joined by `ASKS` in `joinOperands`); 30 brief touch strokes from the hand landing before the pen were kept as ink and as records (now the brush rule, and a Finger: Ignored setting); his 4 read as y, ( ) and 1 in several records (4x+y−x, 3.1()·2) and his 6 as 4 again — the fine-tune's first job. `widths` was added to records so the next file says what the browser reports for his palm.
- The fourth training file (7 Nov night, prompts 38–60; `test/fixtures/training_2026-11-07d.json`): the drill finished (60 gold rows over the four files); all eight two-line and three-line prompts matched first time and no palm record was kept (the brush rule). The Tab S9+ reports no contact size at all (`widths` all null), so on it palms are caught by timing alone (the pen window, the resting hand, the brush rule), never by the 28 px rule. Its misreads (the small and large 144·69, the closed-4 prompt's first try) went into the v4 fine-tune.
- Letter/number look-alikes without context: `q`/`9`, `s`/`5`, `z`/`2`, `b`/`6`, `P`/`p`, `V`/`v`. The school prior leans toward numbers; with `symbols: 'all'` pass `prior: 0`.
- Some two-stroke `4`s are still split into `1 +`; some small radicals read as `r`/`v`.
- Subscripts are deliberately hard to trigger (rare in middle school); `\lim`, `\sum` and `\int` limits are not laid out.
- A late stroke that does not touch its symbol (the two halves of an x written far apart in time and not meeting) is still read on its own.
- `%` and `$` are learned from synthetic samples only (CROHME has neither).
- The views draw what was read: a misread symbol is tidied, re-written or typeset as what it was read as (a correction redraws it). Answers are worked out from what was read, so a misread symbol gives a wrong (gold) answer until it is fixed; the answer itself is never wrong for what was read (`test/solve_check.js` §12).
- Solve does not solve cubics or higher (beyond x³ = q), quadratics with π or another approximation in their coefficients (beyond x² = p), quadratic inequalities, rearranging with π in it (C = 2πr, r =), with the letter squared or underneath (A = πr², r =), or with a bottom of several terms that might cancel; a square root that does not come out in x² = p is still a cut decimal (±7.071…) while a full quadratic gives a radical ((−3 ± √5)/2), which is a deliberate inconsistency kept from 1.4. Two equations beside each other that are the same line (x + y = 1, 2x + 2y = 2) answer "x =" with −y + 1 (true of both) rather than nothing. Repeating decimals are read only from LaTeX (the reader makes no bar-over-digits node), and subscripted letters (x₁, y₂) only when the reader takes the subscript, which it rarely does. The letter `l` (A = lw, P = 2l + 2w) is usually read as e, 1 or 2, so those formulas are answered for what was read. It does not solve equations written far from their "x =" (a board keeps each cluster of writing to itself); it does no units or money and evaluates no functions. An answer that would run off the right edge of the board is squeezed to fit after its "=" (to no less than 0.6 of the writing), or else written under its line at full size, ending at the edge (`Pad._fitAnswers`; laid out again when the board changes size); it does not look for other writing in the way. Limits of `\sum`, `\int` and `\lim` are not laid out, so they are not either.
- My hand uses one drawing of each character (the Replicator's average of the box and the sentences, where there are sentences), so repeated letters look identical. Fraction bars are the writer's minus sign stretched, brackets their own brackets made taller, and roots their `√` with the overline redrawn to cover the body; a character missing from the hand (or written in no Replicator box, like `\sum`) is drawn tidied.
- **Board input on real hardware.** The pointer rules were tested with synthetic pointer events and Chrome's emulated touch, and so far on the Tab S9+ only through its training files (the S Pen arrives as `pen`; his palm's taps came through as 1-px touches — hence the tap-beside-the-pen and resting-hand rules), not on the ActivPanel. Whether the panel reports a touch's `width`/`height` (the palm rule needs it), whether its pen arrives as `pen` or as `touch`, and what `buttons` its eraser end sends are unknown: the overlay on a long press of "Nib." shows exactly that, and the 28 px, 350 ms and 1 s thresholds may need tuning from it. Pen hover keeps touch ignored only where the pen sends hover events.
- **Reveal edges.** A revealed answer is keyed to the strokes of its line and the answer: adding any stroke to the line hides it, and erasing one and undoing brings it back shown. `pad.answers` and Copy LaTeX include answers still waiting for a tap. A page's undo history is lost on reload (the pages themselves are kept).
- **Rail at narrow widths.** The top or bottom rail is one row from 1280 px up (labels show from 1880 px, the view names from 1500, the mark from 1320 since the Lasso joined the tools); narrower than that it wraps (two rows on a portrait tablet). A side rail is two columns of icons, one on a screen 1,440 px or taller.
- **The canvas.** No rotation of elements; no grouping or locking (a picture under the writing can be moved with Select — though the eraser leaves it alone); no PDF import (a worksheet goes in as a picture: a screenshot pasted, or a file); text is one font (DM Sans) in a size and color, no bold or lists. Answers are fitted to the view when they are worked out; a PDF of another page is drawn by the out-of-sight pad at the board's size, so its answers come out as on the board unless the page was last looked at on a different screen (then one near the right edge can sit differently). Text typed on the board is one undo step when it is ended, not letter by letter. Pictures live in this browser's IndexedDB: clearing site data loses them (Save the board keeps them in the file). The paper's labels are 12 screen px at 100% (growing to 21 px zoomed in): small from the back of a room on the panel at 100% (`PAPER_LABEL`); the coordinate plane shape's labels grow with its grid.
- CROHME is adults writing on tablets; children writing with fingers on Chromebooks will be messier (the stress sets imitate some of it). Collecting a few hundred consented, anonymous samples from the pad (strokes + the corrected LaTeX) and fine-tuning would be the biggest single improvement; the correction memory already captures part of this per device. The Board's training log (above) now collects them; no network has been fine-tuned on it yet.
