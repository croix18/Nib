# Nib — handoff

Read this before changing anything. It is the project memory: what Nib is, how it works, how it was trained and tuned, how to rebuild it, and where it is weak.

## What it is

Nib turns live handwriting (pen, finger or mouse strokes) into LaTeX, in the browser, with no network and no dependencies. It ships as one file, `dist/nib.js` (about 1 MB including the models and the typeset fonts), so it can be pasted into any single-file tool (Deckhand, Cadence, Tally, Boards Up, the practice tests) and run from Drive over `file://` on the classroom Chromebox. `dist/Nib.html` is a self-contained tester with everything (fonts, KaTeX) inlined. Since 1.2 it can also show what it read in place — the writer's strokes tidied, re-drawn in their Replicator handwriting, or typeset where they were written — and `dist/Board.html` is a whiteboard built on that. Since 1.3 it can also work out any line that ends with "=" and write the answer after it, in the writer's own hand; since 1.4 it solves equations ("x =" under one), and it can treat x as a letter only (`times: false`, the Board's setting), with a raised dot or brackets for multiplication. 1.5 is the classroom-panel round: pen, finger and palm rules with two fingers at once, answers behind a tap-to-reveal chip, pages, save as image, a movable rail, settings, and the Algebra 1 solver (quadratics, rearranging a formula for a letter, repeating decimals).

It reads ink, not photos. A photo of paper is a different (much harder) problem and is out of scope.

## Pipeline

1. **Strokes in.** Arrays of `[x, y]` (or `{x, y}`, or `{points}`), y down, any units. Consecutive duplicate points dropped. With `smooth` > 0 each stroke is smoothed along its path (Gaussian, sigma = `smooth`·`ref`, ends fixed). It is off by default: with the v2 network, 0.03 lifted finger-written expressions from 80% to 89% exact on the stress set, but the v3 network reads finger ink at 90.6% unsmoothed and smoothing only takes a little off that (89.3%).
2. **Scale.** `ref` = median over strokes of max(width, height). Every threshold below is in units of `ref`. The classifier sees a size reference that is held steady while the same ink keeps growing (changes only when the writing's scale moves more than ~15%), so earlier symbols are not re-classified after each stroke.
3. **Segmentation.** Dynamic programme over time-ordered strokes (a symbol is 1–4 consecutive strokes whose boxes stay within 1.2·`ref`; only 0.5% of CROHME symbols break time order). A group is scored by the network's not-junk probability (weight `wJunk`; the network has a `junk` class trained on stroke groups that span two symbols), its best class probability (`wClass`), the stroke-join model (P(stroke k and k+1 belong together) from 16 geometric features, weight `wJoin`) and a per-symbol cost (`beta`). Boundaries the join model is very sure of are not crossed (`prune`). Two rules: a tiny mark at the baseline corner of a digit or letter is its own decimal point (`dotPen`), and two tall arcs after an open bracket are `)(` not `x` (`parenPen`). The DP keeps label states, so an optional writing-order context term (`lmSeg`) can score labels while segmenting; it did not help in tuning and is off. **Late strokes** (`delayed`): the DP keeps time order, so a stroke added to an earlier symbol much later (crossing a t at the end, the bar of a + or the second half of an x after the next number) would read as a symbol of its own. Before segmenting, a stroke that touches or crosses ink written two or more strokes earlier (within `lateTol`·`ref`), but neither the stroke just before it nor the one after it, is moved to just after the latest stroke it touches, so the DP sees the symbol's strokes together. After segmenting, two symbols that are not neighbours in time are still merged when their boxes overlap and the network reads the union as one confident symbol that scores `delayGain` better than the two apart; a dot joins only a symbol it sits just above or below (the dot of an i, the dots of ÷), never one it sits beside (a decimal point stays a decimal point). On the stress set where one stroke of a symbol is written last, this takes exact readings from 31% (1.0) to 85.5%.
4. **Classifier.** Each group is seen two ways. (a) Rendered to 4 orientation channels (0/45/90/135°, undirected, so stroke order and direction do not matter) on a 32×32 grid → 5 conv layers (24-48-48-96-96) with 3 max-pools. (b) Its pen path resampled to 48 points (x, y, direction, new-stroke flag) → three 1-D conv layers (32-64-64), max- and mean-pooled. Both, plus 5 scalars (aspect, size relative to `ref`, stroke count, ink length), feed FC 192 → 104 classes (101 CROHME symbols + `%` + `$` + `junk`). Int8 weights with per-channel scales, dequantised at load. `rasterize()` / `onlineFeatures()` in `core.js` mirror `training/raster.py` / `training/online.py` (parity: max pixel difference 1/255; probabilities within 0.016 of the float model; top-1 agreement 299/300).
5. **School prior.** The network learned CROHME's symbol frequencies (university maths: many letters). With `symbols: 'school'` each class probability is re-weighted by (frequency in a middle-school corpus / frequency in CROHME)^`prior` (table `DATA.prior.school`, built by `training/prior.py`; ratios run from 0.2 for rare-in-school symbols to 5.2 for digits).
6. **Symbol fix-ups.** Two stacked dots → `:`; `-` over `-` → `=`; `+` over `-` → `±` (only when nothing sits directly above or below the pair, so fraction bars are safe).
7. **Layout.** In order:
   - square roots, largest first: body = units right of the check mark's lowest point and under the overline (grows when a nested root is found; fraction bars under it and units starting below it are excluded); index = a small unit up and to the left;
   - fraction bars, widest first: a `-` with units clearly above and below it within reach (bar line fitted through the stroke, so sloped bars work), numerator/denominator grown by stacking and by running past the bar ends; operators at the very ends of the bar and units that straddle it are excluded; rejected unless both sides are roughly centred on the bar;
   - lines: units grouped into horizontal bands; a band of up to four small units, each sitting just above and right of a symbol in the line below (or right after the previous one), is merged back as exponents — anything else is a new line (so a short answer line like `88` stays its own line);
   - absolute value: two tall straight strokes read as `1`/brackets are paired into `| |` when both hang below the line of characters and the classifier finds them bar-like (`barH`, `barW`), or when two `1`s stand exactly where bars would: around a plain operand (digits or letters, no relation inside), each at an operand boundary (after an operator, a relation, an open bracket or the start; before one or the end);
   - baselines: units sorted by centre x; each unit is Right / Sup / Sub of the current baseline unit by the relation model (MLP over box geometry, typography class and digit/letter flags) with hard rules: operators and open brackets take no scripts; relations are never scripts; a leading minus joins the exponent it starts; a digit after a digit is an exponent only when clearly raised; a cross between two numbers is a times sign and takes no subscript.
8. **Slope.** After the first layout, the writing's slope is measured from the symbols just read, in writing order: neighbouring digits and letters of a similar size for their kind (an x-height letter counts as 0.62 of a digit) give the line through their bottoms, signs give the line through their middles; pairs steeper than 17° are dropped (exponents, the next line), the rest vote with weight growing with distance, and at least two must agree. When the agreed slope is more than `deskew` degrees, the same symbols are sheared level and laid out again (they are not re-read; boxes are reported around the ink as written) and `result.skew` says by how much. On the stress sets this lifts drifting lines from 84% to 89% and turned lines from 85% to 87% (v2 network), multi-line work most of all, while straight writing reads exactly as before (the composed set and the real 2013 set unchanged).
9. **Personal memory.** `Pad.relabel` (the "did you mean" menu) stores the corrected symbol's 192-number embedding with its label in localStorage (`nib-memory-v1`, 400 max). A later symbol whose embedding is nearly identical (cosine 0.88 → 0.96 ramps the pull from 0 to 0.8) has its probabilities pulled toward the remembered label. Ink itself is not stored. `Nib.memory.clear()` forgets. Calibrated with `test/memcal.js` on the 2013 writers: corrections fixed 170 later mistakes and disturbed 49 correct readings (5,627 symbols).
10. **Context model.** Viterbi over each row's symbol candidates with a bigram model (CROHME rows + 30,000 synthetic middle-school expressions, weight `lm`), then a final layout pass if any label changed.
11. **Post-layout.** A dot on the baseline is a decimal point, a raised dot is `\cdot`; a small raised circle after a digit or `)` is a degree sign (`degrees`); `x` between two numbers is `\times`.
12. **Output.** LaTeX (KaTeX/MathJax-ready), plain text, per-line tokens, the tree, each symbol with box and candidates, a confidence.

The pad reads in time slices: `Recognizer.warmup()` classifies the stroke groups the DP will need one at a time (newest ink first), then rehearses the whole reading against the cache — the DP and the late-stroke merges, with any group not yet classified noted and read as junk for the moment — classifies what the rehearsal asked for, and repeats until a rehearsal needs nothing new (a late-stroke merge round depends on the answers of the one before). The pad runs it ~14 ms per slice between frames, so the final `recognize()` only reads the cache (checked by `test/check.js`: nothing classified after a warm-up, and the same reading as a cold recognizer); new ink cancels a read in progress. `Nib.recognizeAsync()` does the same for whole expressions. On a board (`separate`), strokes are grouped into expressions first (neighbours on a line: within 1.5 `ref` across and 0.6 up or down; stacked: within 1.6 down and 0.3 across), each expression is read by its own recognizer and kept while its strokes and corrections do not change, and the readings are joined (`\begin{gathered}`, one line per expression).

## In-place views (`src/neat.js`)

A view never changes the reading; it lays the reading out again where it was written.

1. **Units.** `planOf(result, strokes, provider)` works in em of the writer's size: em = the median height of the digits and capitals / 0.7 (small letters count as 0.48 em, `g p q y` as 0.71). Cap height 0.7, x-height 0.48, descender 0.23, maths axis 0.25 em (`NEAT`).
2. **Level.** The slope the recognizer measured (`result.skew`) is taken out the same way (shear about the ink's mean x), and each line's baseline is the median bottom of its sized symbols.
3. **Layout**, TeX-like, per line: spacing by atom kind (thin/medium/thick: 1/6, 2/9, 5/18 em) or, where the writer left their own gaps between digits and neighbours, those gaps (`gapsOf`); exponents raised at 0.65 size; fractions centred on the axis at 0.85 size; roots that cover their body (index at 0.6); brackets as tall as what they hold. A gap wider than 1.6 em splits a line into parts that each start where they were written; each part keeps its written width (`fitRow`: the gaps stretch from 0.5 to 2.5 times, typeset glyphs may shrink to 0.8). Relations written one under another on several lines are aligned to their median position.
4. **Providers** give each symbol a glyph in em: **tidy** (`tidyProvider`) fits the writer's own strokes to the symbol's kind (height, width or larger side; sitting on the baseline, hanging, or centred on the axis — `sizeSpec`), smooths the wobble, makes nearly straight strokes straight and nearly level or upright ones exact (`tidyPaths`), squares signs (`squareSign`: `=` bars level, equal and stacked; `+` upright through its bar's middle; `÷` dots over the middle) and keeps a root's tick while redrawing its overline to cover the body (`tidyRadical`). Point counts are kept so ink can morph into it. **hand** (`handProvider`) uses the Replicator's strokes for the character (box space → em as the font does: 1.1 em wide, baseline 0.95 em below the top of a 1.25 em box), with tidy stand-ins for characters the writer never wrote. **typeset** (`typeProvider`) uses KaTeX's metrics and fonts (`DATA.tsfm`, `DATA.tsfont`: Main, Math italic for letters, Size1; registered at runtime as "Nib KaTeX …" with the system's maths fonts as fallback), rules for fraction bars, a drawn radical, `≠` as `=` with a stroke through it.
5. **Items.** `drawablesOf(plan)` turns the plan into items `{key, src (stroke indices), label, box, parts}` in ink coordinates with parts `ink` (paths), `text`, `rule` and `line`; `drawItems(ctx, items)` draws them. `Nib.neaten` / `Nib.typeset` return these for use outside a pad.
6. **In a pad.** After each reading the pad builds a plan per expression and ties every item to its stroke objects (`from`), so the eraser takes away what it touches as shown, undo restores it, and items survive edits elsewhere. Switching views (or a new hand) re-plans without re-reading; items morph point by point when the stroke counts match and otherwise cross-fade between boxes (260 ms, off with `motion="false"` or reduced motion).
7. **The saved hand.** `Nib.hand.load()` keeps a Replicator file in localStorage (`nib-hand-v1`, dated). `handLoad()` also looks at the Replicator's own saved work (`replicator-v1`, dated since 1.2): the newer of the two wins, so letters redone in the Replicator are never hidden by an older file. Both offline pages opened from one computer in Chrome share that storage (the storage event updates an open board); published pages each have their own, so there the file is the way across.

## Answers after "=" (`src/solve.js`, drawn by `src/neat.js`)

1. **Tokens** (`tokensOf`): the reading tree becomes numbers (digits and one point; a comma before exactly three digits is a thousands comma), operators (× and ⋅ are times, ÷ and / divide), relations, brackets, absolute-value bars, letters (with subscripts), π, fractions, roots, superscripts, % and !; a bar (`over` node, LaTeX `\overline`) over digits straight after a number with a point makes it a repeating decimal (`rep` on the number token; `qOfRepeating`: 0.1 with 6 repeating is (16 − 1)/90), and a bar over anything else means no answer. The handwriting reader makes no `over` nodes, so this is LaTeX only for now. Anything else (∑, sin, $, ±, ∞) means no answer, and so does a run of letters that spells a unit or word (`SOLVE_WORDS`: cm, ft, GCF, …). Classroom readings: where × can be a times sign (`times` not false), an x between a number, %, closing bracket or bar, fraction, power, root or ! and a number or fraction is a times sign ("3 x 4", "2/3 x 6"); "of" after one of those is times ("15% of 80"), and so is "0f" (an o read as a zero) after anything but a number.
2. **Parse** (`solveParse`): + and − over ×, ÷ and implicit products, left to right (6 ÷ 2(1+2) = 9), except that a slash between two whole numbers is a fraction (2/3x = ⅔x) and a term with letters after ÷ or / is divided by as a whole (6x ÷ 3x = 2, C ÷ 2π); a lone letter just before a bracket of numbers is function notation (f(2)) and is refused; unary minus below powers (−2² = −4), powers right-associative, then ! and %. A whole number followed by a fraction of whole numbers is a mixed number; any other number or fraction straight after something without an operation (x4, (2)3, 15%80, ½ 0) is refused.
3. **Evaluate** (`solveEval`): coefficients are exact fractions `{n, d}` (BigInt, at most 4096 bits) or floats `{f}` (π, roots that do not come out, fractional powers of non-perfect powers); values are polynomials `Map(monomial → coefficient)` (at most 64 terms, degree 24), so letters are combined and multiplied out, and a polynomial divides by a single term when every term divides (6x² ÷ 3x = 2x). A float result below a billion within 1e-13 (relative) of a whole number, a fraction with denominator ≤ 1000 or a decimal of six significant digits becomes exact (√2·√2 = 2, π·7² ÷ (π·50²) = 0.0196), never zero; π and roots that do not come out are made raw and never snap. A float sum that cancels to within 1e-10 of its parts is refused (π − 3.14159265358979: zero or not cannot be told), and so is one that comes out exactly zero from an exact number and an approximation (π − 3.141592653589793); two approximations that cancel exactly (π − π) give 0. Dividing by letters, powers with letters in them, roots or bars around letters: no answer. `info` records how the question was asked (decimals, %, fractions stacked or with a slash, mixed numbers, ÷, negative powers, π, a given value put in).
4. **Form**: a lone number turns into its other form (decimal → fraction or mixed number; proper fraction → decimal; improper fraction in lowest terms → mixed number; unreduced fraction → reduced; mixed number → improper fraction; % → decimal; a lone whole number gets nothing; a fraction with a slash counts as a fraction, a sign inside one as a sign on it). Percents: a sum of percents, or anything × 100%, answers as a percent; a percent added to anything else is refused. Otherwise: approximate → three decimal places (three significant digits below 1) cut, never rounded, and an ellipsis; decimals or % in the question → a decimal (exact up to 12 places; repeating with a bar when the repeat is at most 6 digits after at most 6; else four places cut and an ellipsis); fractions → a fraction, or a mixed number when the question had one; ÷ of whole numbers → a decimal; else a fraction. Letters: standard form (degree, then alphabetical exponents: a² − 2ab + b²); no answer when the expression is already a sum of distinct simplest terms, or one such sum over a whole number with nothing to cancel ((x + 1)/2), unless signs are doubled (x + −3) or a given value goes in (`simplified`).
5. **Lines** (`solveRows`): a line whose last symbol is "=" (and no other relation) is answered, using what lies between it and the "=" before it; every earlier part of a chain must be worked out and agree with it ("2(3 + 4) = 2 · 7 =" → 14; "3 + 4 = 7 + 1 =", "1 ÷ 0 = 2 + 3 =" and "3 + 4 = x + 2x =" → nothing), except that letters before numbers stand for them ("A = lw = 3 · 4 =" → 12). A line "x = …" whose right side works out to a number gives x for later lines of the same expression on a board (each cluster has its own letters, so two problems on one board never share an x). When it ends the working of an equation in x alone on the line just above, the value is soft: it is put in only when there is nothing else to do (x + 1 = → 5, 3x + 2x = → 5x); a value under a formula with several letters (d = rt, then r = 60) is given outright. "x = x + 1" is an equation, not a value, and an equation that the given values do not satisfy makes its letters unknowns again.
6. **Equations** (`solveFor`): every line with one relation (=, <, >, ≤, ≥) and two sides is kept with its two sides' trees; a line with a relation and letters that cannot be kept (|x| = 5, √x, 2ˣ, a chain, a compound inequality, a percent added to a number, a misread line) is kept as a block. A line that is just a letter and a relation ("x =", "x <") takes the nearest line above with that letter that answers its kind of question (a solved "x = 4" check under an inequality is passed over; a block, or any other line in the way, means no answer). Each side is worked out as a fraction of polynomials (`ratEval`), so a letter may sit underneath (3/x = 6/10), with the values given above put in for every letter but the asked one (A = lw, l = 5, w = 3: A = 15); P = left·right-denominator − right·left-denominator. One letter: linear (−b/a), or a·xⁿ + b with n = 2 (±; nothing for a negative; a square root that does not come out is a cut decimal, x² = 50 → ±7.071…) or 3, or a quadratic a·x² + b·x + c with b ≠ 0 and exact coefficients (`quadRoots`: scaled to whole numbers with no common factor and a > 0; D = b² − 4ac; D < 0 no answer; D = 0 one root; D a perfect square two rational roots, smallest first; otherwise (p ± k√m)/d with m square-free and p, k, d sharing no factor, carried as `rad` with float approximations in `vals` for comparing; D over 10¹⁰ is refused); a value that makes a denominator zero is no answer, for any root (`settle`). Answers with two roots are written `-3,\;-2` (kind `roots`, a `,` node between them) and radicals `\frac{-3\pm\sqrt{5}}{2}` or `-2\pm\sqrt{2}` (kind `radical`, a `root` node; `rootsAnswer`); their `value` is null. Working lines are compared as sets of roots (`sameSettle` sorts them). Several letters (at most 3, linear, no letter underneath): all the equations on the lines right next to it in the same piece of writing, solved exactly by elimination (`systemFor`) — if they contradict each other there is no answer, and a letter is answered when they pin it down even if others stay free (y = 0 under two equations in x, y, z); where both the given values and the system speak, they must agree (so a wrong "x = 5" under x + y = 10 and x − y = 2 gets no y). **Rearranging** (`rearrangedFor`, `rearrange`): when neither settles the letter, an equation in several letters (values given above put in first) is solved for it if it appears only to the first power and never in a denominator: P = A·v + B gives v = −B/A, written as a polynomial when A is a number (2x + 3y = 12 → −2/3 x + 4; P = 2l + 2w → 1/2 P − w: the canonical form is terms, with a fraction coefficient, like every other expression answer), divided out when A is one term that divides every term of B (xy + xz = x → −z + 1), else one fraction scaled to whole-number coefficients with no common factor and a positive first term underneath (A = lw → A/l, y = mx + b → (y − b)/m, A = ½bh → 2A/b). Terms are in standard order except that a plus term goes first among those of the highest degree (y − b, not −b + y). No answer when the line is already "v = …" with no v on the right (y = 2x + 1, then y =), when B is 0 and A has letters (xy = 0: y = 0 only if x ≠ 0), when a bottom of several terms shares a letter with the top (it might cancel: (a + b)y = a² − b²), with π or another approximation in it, or for an inequality. The lines right next to it in the same piece of writing must each rearrange to the same thing (its working: 2x + 3y = 12, 3y = −2x + 12); a line beside it with its letters but not v, or one that rearranges differently, means it may be a system, and there is no answer. The answer is kind `expression`. The value's form: `styleOf` for a value given outright ("x = 10 ÷ 4" → 2.5), otherwise decimals in the equation give a decimal, mixed numbers a mixed number, anything else a fraction (`eqStyleOf`). The working right above a one-letter line must settle to the same thing line by line (a slip in a step gets no answer), up to a solved line ("x = 4") that ends an earlier problem. Inequalities: one letter, linear, nothing underneath; the relation flips with a negative coefficient, and the number is given only when the relation written after the letter is the right one. ± answers have kind `plusminus`. On a board, an "x =" whose piece of writing has no line with x may use the equations of the piece just above it (within four writing heights, overlapping across).
7. **Drawing**: answers are reading-tree nodes (digits, '.', '-', ±, %, letters with powers, fractions, `over` for the repeating bar, three points for the ellipsis) laid out by `placeAnswers` after the "=" (or <, >, ≤, ≥) item on the line's baseline at the writer's em, with TeX's space after a relation plus the writer's own gap. Glyphs come from `sampleBook`: the clearest instance (p ≥ 0.6) of that symbol anywhere on the board, else the Replicator hand, else for a letter any instance of it on the board, else a built-in hand (`DEFAULT_HAND`: Catmull-Rom control points for 0–9 . − + ± , and a–z, A–Z), else any instance of it. Roots in answers (`root` nodes) are drawn by the view's radical (`synthRadical` in tidy when there is no ink to take it from, the hand's √, or the typeset one). In the Ink view only the answers are laid out, after the raw "=", and sheared back onto the line's slope (`unlevel`). Points in tidy views are drawn as dots of 0.042 em so a decimal point is never lost.
8. **x as a letter** (`times: false`, the Board's setting): `allowedMask` drops × from the symbol set and `foldTimes` gives the network's × score to x (so a cross written like × is an x, never a +), and the solver never turns an x into a times sign; multiplication is a raised dot or brackets. Recognition helps both: a dot halfway up its neighbours is heard by the context model as a times dot (it would otherwise rather hear x − x than x . x), a tiny mark beside a digit or letter (baseline or middle) is never part of it (121·21 is not 12121), and a ")" with no "(" (or a "(" never closed) takes the bracket from the 1 most likely to be one (`balanceBrackets`).
9. **Misreadings that must not become answers**: two symbols one above the other read as neighbours on a line (a ½ whose bar was missed, read as −12), or a power or index straight above or below its base, are marked `doubt` by `markStacked` and get no answer; so does a plus sign straight after another sign (5 + + 2x). A dot level with a fraction bar between fractions is a times dot. On a board, writing that carries on past a gap on the same line after a sign (the left piece ends with + − × ÷ ·, or the right one begins with + × ÷ · =, within six writing heights) is worked out as one problem ("13.999 +   2 + 7 =" → 22.999); two problems on one line stay two.
10. **In the pad**: `solve` is a view option (no re-read). Answer items carry no strokes (`from: []`) but the "=" strokes they follow (`eqFrom`); they are drawn in `--nib-answer`, glide between views by id, fade out when they go, vanish at once when their "=" is erased, and step aside the moment new ink (even a stroke in progress) reaches their box. With `reveal` they are laid out the same way but drawn as one gold chip per answer (x-height across, where the answer starts) until tapped. `pad.answers` lists `{line, latex, text, kind, exact, value}`; `solvedLatex()` writes them in.

## Replicator (`replicator/core.js`, `replicator/template.html`)

- **Outlines.** Each stroke is resampled every 6 font units; hooks go first (`dehook`: a turn of more than 130° within 12 units of an end at smoothing 1–2, 20 units from 3 up, and never more than an eighth of the stroke: a pen landing or lifting, while the flag of a 1, about three times as long, stays); then a Gaussian along the path (`SMOOTH_SIGMA`, σ 0–78 units for strengths 0–10; default 5, and files saved before strengths went to 10 move up by 3) applied piece by piece between corners (a turn over 1.2 rad stays sharp), the ends held; then the stroke is swept with a round pen (`weight`) and traced. **Even out letter sizes** (`fitToGuides`) scales a character towards the height its kind should have (cap, x-height; descenders hang from the x-height line) within 0.7–1.4.
- **Sentences.** The ruled lines are boxes laid end to end (line units: the box height; `toLine`/`toBox`). `alignSentence(lines, text, templates)` matches the strokes to the text by dynamic programming over (strokes used, characters written): a character is 1–4 consecutive strokes on one line, scored by a greedy point-cloud distance (`cloud`/`cloudDist`, the $P recogniser's, 24 points: stroke order and direction do not matter) to its box drawing and its last three learned samples; a character can be left out or a stroke be stray at a fixed cost; the late strokes of i, j and t (small marks written back to the left, after the word) are set aside and given afterwards to the letter whose ink they sit over; a beam (1.6) keeps it to about 0.3 s for a long sentence. A letter is learned only if its distance is under `accept` (0.085) and clearly nearer its own character than any other (0.8 of the nearest rival); a sentence teaches nothing at all if under 75% of its letters got strokes, the mean distance is over 1.3 × accept, or under 35% of the letters that have a box drawing were learned (the wrong sentence: 0 letters over 48 tries; sentences written far less like the boxes are still learned from). `averageGlyph` takes the box drawing and up to 12 samples per character, keeps those with the commonest stroke count, drops outliers (over 2.2 × the median distance to the medoid), matches strokes by order and direction, centres them on the medoid and averages 40 points per stroke. The page keeps samples per character in its saved work (and the `.json`), and the averages beside the box drawings as `avg`, which Nib's `handOf` uses for My hand.

## Training data and models

- **CROHME** (online handwritten maths expressions, InkML with symbol-level segmentation and MathML ground truth). CROHME 2019 from the GitHub mirror `Mahdiyeh-Asgharpour/CROHME2019` (train 8,901; CROHME 2014 test 986; CROHME 2019 test 1,199) and the CROHME 2012 and 2013 test sets from `ThomasLech/CROHME_extractor` (488 + 671, not part of the 2019 release). Splits: **train** = 2019 train + 2014 test + 2012 test (10,375 expressions, ~102k symbols); **tuning** = 2013 test (671) and `synth_t13` (700 school expressions composed from the 2013 writers' symbols); **reported test** = 2019 test (1,199) and `test/school_synth.jsonl` (700 composed from the 2019 test writers), never used for training or tuning. CROHME is distributed for research use; the models here are derived from it.
- Classifier v2: 14 epochs over six augmented renderings + one clean, AdamW, cosine schedule, label smoothing, class weights; 90.4% symbol accuracy (99.0% top-3) on the 2013 writers over all 101 classes. v1 (a smaller raster-only CNN on less data) reached 59.7% / 75.7% on the two tuning sets where v2 reaches 61.8% / 80.6% before tuning.
- Classifier v3 (shipped in 1.1): v2 fine-tuned for 6 more epochs (`training/train_v3.py`), half of them rendered with `training/aug3.py` on top of v2's augmentation: strokes drawn backwards (30%), the strokes of a symbol in another order (35%), mouse-like sparse pixel-snapped points (20%) and finger-like wobble (20%). On the 2013 writers' symbols it keeps v2's accuracy on clean ink (90.4%) and reads the same symbols rewritten messily far better (87.4% vs 82.0%). End to end it raised every stress set (finger 80% → 91%), the composed 2019-writer set (90.2% → 91.5% on the stress tool's sample) and the real 2019 school set, and is slightly lower on the 2013 tuning sets (real 63.4% → 60.7%, composed 89.1% → 88.6%, mostly single letters of ambiguous case: s/S, v/V, w/u). The epoch-4 model and an average of epochs 4–6 were both weaker overall. Remembered corrections from 1.0 do not carry over (the memory is keyed to the network).
- Join and relation MLPs (scikit-learn): 95.3% and 97.1% on the 2013 writers.
- **Training log** (the Board's "Keep writing for training", `train` on a pad). A piece of writing (one part of a board, as `clustersOf` groups it) is kept once it has been read and left alone for 4 s — no stroke added to it or near it, nothing erased in it — or when the page it is on is left (another page, Clear, the page deleted, `pagehide`); a Fix keeps its piece again at once. Its key is its strokes' ids, a hash of each stroke's points at 0.1 px (the precision pages are saved at), so the same writing has the same key after a reload or on a page taken out again, and keeping a key again replaces its record. A record: `{key, t (ms since 1970), w, h (the board in CSS px), view, strokes: [[[x, y, t], ...]] (from the piece's top-left, 0.1 px, t in ms from its first point; 0 for strokes put back from storage), types: ['pen' | 'touch' | 'mouse' | null per stroke], latex (the reading as shown, corrections in), symbols: [{label, strokes: [indices into strokes]}], fixed: [{strokes: [indices], label}] (the writer's corrections), answered (an answer was worked out for one of its lines, shown or behind its chip), confidence}`; at most 3,000 records and about 4 MB, the oldest dropped first. A saved file is `{format: 'nib-training', version: 1, exported (ISO date), engine (Nib's version), records}`. **To fine-tune from it:** (1) `python3 -I tools/training_to_jsonl.py Nib-training-*.json training/scratch/data/board.jsonl --repeat-fixed 3` (rows `{id, writer: 'board', truth, strokes: [[x, y], ...] (y down, like CROHME), symbols, orphans, latex, source: 'board', types, fixed, split: 'board'}`; a times dot becomes `.`, `:` two dots, a degree sign `o`, and a label the classifier has no class for is left out; the same record in several files is taken once; fixed rows written 3 times weigh the corrections more); (2) add `'board'` to the train sources in `training/build_samples_v2.py` (`('train', ('train', 'valid', 't12', 'board'))`) and run it; (3) re-render the epochs (`make_epochs_v2.py 6 2`, `make_epochs_v3.py 4 2`; both skip files that exist, so move the old `ep2/` and `ep3/` `.npz` away first); (4) run the fine-tune script, `python3 train_v3.py scratch/work/model_v4.npz` (6 epochs from the v2 checkpoint; give it a new name, as it resumes from `<out>.ckpt`); (5) `python3 ../build.py scratch/work/model_v4.npz`, `node ../test/parity.js scratch/work/model_v4.npz` and the measurements in `training/README.md` §4, which must not drop. Symbols nobody fixed carry the reader's own guess, so a misreading that was not corrected teaches the mistake: the more corrected the log, the better, and a few hundred expressions is a start, not a retrain. Remembered corrections (`nib-memory-v1`) do not carry over to a new network.
- **Training drill** (the Board, Settings → Training → Start the drill; `board/template.html`, `DRILL`). Free writing only ever has the reader's own guess as its label; the drill gives the whole expression a true label. Drill `v1` is 60 prompts stored as LaTeX with `how` ('any' | 'pen' | 'finger') and an optional `note` ('small', 'large', 'fast', 'your usual 4', 'a closed 4', 'fraction small and a little off-centre'): arithmetic, decimals and %, fractions and mixed numbers, powers, roots and absolute value, like terms and brackets, two- and three-line equations with "x =" under them, inequalities and ≠, brackets for times, the same expression small/large/fast, two kinds of 4, and five for the finger. Starting turns training on, goes to a new page (unless the page is empty) and shows a bar across the top of the surface (an overlay under the rail, on every rail position; the pad keeps its size, so nothing is refitted). The prompt is drawn with KaTeX when `window.katex` exists, otherwise (both Board builds: KaTeX is not in them) as plain text in the typeset fonts (`typeFontsReady`): fractions a/b with "(stacked)", √ and ∛, superscript digits, letters in the maths italic. While a prompt shows, `pad.trainMeta = {expect, drill: 'v1', item, how, note?}`. The reading line waits until the board has read everything and the newest stroke ended 1 s ago; it takes the piece (one part of `clustersOf`) whose lines, joined with ` \\ `, match the prompt, else the newest piece, and shows it with its answers, gold when `normTex(reading) === normTex(expect)` (whitespace, `\left`/`\right` and `\,` spacing dropped; `·` and `⋅` = `\cdot`; `\dfrac` = `\frac`, `\frac12` = `\frac{1}{2}`, `x^2` = `x^{2}`; `\leq` = `\le` and the like; `\%` = `%`; one final `=` optional), coral otherwise. A finger prompt whose piece has a stroke from a pen or a mouse (`strokeInfo`) gets a coral border and says so; it is still kept. **Right**: `trainKeep(piece)`, `trainVerify(key, true)`, then `trainMeta = null` and Clear (anything else on the page is kept as free writing, without the prompt), next prompt. **Fix**: Fix mode; `relabel` keeps the piece again with `fixed`, the reading line follows, and Right keeps it fixed and verified. **Skip**: `trainIgnore()` and Clear, the item noted as skipped. **Stop**: bar hidden, `trainMeta` null, the place kept. Turning training off stops the drill. Progress: `nib-drill-v1` = `{v: 1, drill, pos (0-based; N = finished), skipped: [items], done: [record keys], fixed: [items], active}`, written at each step; `active` brings the bar back after a reload. After the last prompt the bar shows "d done, s skipped, f fixed" (done = Right presses; the skipped items listed) with Save training and Start again. **Records** from the drill carry, besides the usual fields, `expect` (the prompt's LaTeX), `drill`, `item` (1-based), `how`, `note` (when the prompt has one) and, after Right, `verified: true`; records made while a prompt showed but not verified (kept after 4 s, or on a page change) have `expect` but no `verified`. **The converter** copies those fields into each row, adds `gold: true` when `verified` is true and the reading equals `expect` under the same normalisation (`norm_tex`, kept in step with `normTex`), `gold: false` otherwise, prints the number of gold rows, and with `--verified-only` writes only verified rows. **Using gold rows in fine-tuning**: a gold row's symbols and structure are what the writer meant, checked by the writer, so it is the one kind of board row that can (a) be trusted for the symbol classifier without the "uncorrected misreading teaches the mistake" caveat above — give gold rows the weight fixed rows get (`--repeat-fixed`, or write them twice), and drop or down-weight non-gold drill rows (`--verified-only` drops the unchecked; a verified row that is not gold means the writer accepted a reading other than the prompt, so treat it like free writing); (b) train the join and relation models and the context model, which need true expression structure; and (c) above all serve as an expression-level held-out set from this writer's real hand (finger and pen apart, by `how` and `types`): keep a fixed share of drill items (for example every fifth item, or one whole run of the drill) out of training and report exact-match on it next to the measurements in `training/README.md` §4, before and after each fine-tune. The `item` lets the same prompt be compared across runs and across pen and finger.

Messy writing (`node test/stress.js` rewrites the composed set; each variant scored on every third expression, `EVERY=3 SYNTH=training/scratch/data/stress_<variant>.jsonl node test/eval_synth.js`), 1.0 → 1.1: neat 88.5 → 91.5, strokes drawn backwards 83.8 → 90.2, strokes of a symbol reordered 83.3 → 87.6, one stroke added at the end 31.2 → 85.5, mouse 87.6 → 90.2, finger 78.2 → 90.6, line turned 6–12° 82.9 → 88.0, line drifting 6–12° 82.1 → 90.2, two or three at once 62.8 → 83.3. In 1.1 the network (v3) accounts for most of the backwards, reordered, finger and mixed gains, the late-stroke stage for the late strokes, the slope stage for turned and drifting lines (and the multi-line category), and the absolute-value rule for `| |` (46% → 83% on the composed set).

In 1.4 the gains are in arithmetic and powers: a raised dot is no longer heard as a minus by the context model, a dot is no longer swallowed by the digit beside it, brackets back to back (")(") are no longer read as an x when they are as tall as the digits, and an unpaired bracket takes back the "(" or ")" that was read as a 1. On the Board's own convention (x a letter, · and brackets for times: 17 algebra and arithmetic problems × 30 composed writers), 1.3 read 85.9% right and gave a wrong answer 4.9% of the time; 1.4 reads 91.4% right and gives a wrong answer 2.4% of the time (`2x(x+5)` 29/30, `x·x` 17 → 25/30, `(121)(21)` 14 → 23/30). Equations with "x =" under them (12 kinds × 12 writers): 90% answered right, 1.4% wrong.

1.4.1 came from a real board (Croix's screenshot of 4 Nov, replayed with the tools above; the replay gave back the same three wrong answers his board had shown). Three changes, none of which moves any figure above (composed, school and full sets identical to the expression; the convention and equation probes identical): a **lopsided fraction** — one side squarely on the bar, the other written off to one side of it, such as a ½ whose 2 runs out past the end of a short bar, or whose 1 sits high over its left end — is still a fraction (`takeFractions`: the off side is taken if it overlaps 30% of the bar and its middle is within 0.6 bar widths + 0.45 ref of the bar's); a **lone bracket** standing like the digits beside it inside a number, with 1 as a real second guess (≥ 0.15), is the 1 (a curved 1 in 13 + 4 read as "(3 + 4"; not in powers or indices, and not next to a letter); and a **minus wholly above** the symbol after it is doubted, so the line gets no answer (a ½ whose numerator went to the line above would otherwise be read "7 + −2" and answered 5). On the replayed board 12 of its 13 problems are now answered right and none wrong; the thirteenth, 5x + 2x with its first x written as an upright cross, reads 5 + + 2x and gets no answer (both crosses are pluses to the classifier, 0.98 and 0.95).

The full-set figure is in the range published research models report on this benchmark (most of those read images, not ink, and run on servers). On the tuning sets 1.1 reaches 61.3% (real 2013 school-style) and 88.6% (composed from the 2013 writers); 1.0 reached 63.9% and 87.3%, and the first version of the engine (v1 network, untuned) 59.7% and 75.7%.

Speed (headless Chrome on a 2-core server, shared with other work): the 14-stroke sample is read 0.9 s after navigation including page load; a 6-symbol expression is ready 0.75 s after the last pen-up, of which 0.45 s is the deliberate rest delay. In Node a whole 8–20-stroke expression takes 0.35–1.2 s from cold and about 10 ms once its strokes are cached. One stroke group costs about 25 ms to classify (the CNN dominates), so the views and the Board are dominated by reading, not drawing: with the processor slowed 4×, a new expression written beside 12 others (90 strokes) on the Board shows in 3.5–4.5 s, and no task in that time runs longer than about 0.25 s (`test/board_browser.js`).

## Known weak spots

- The views: a part whose newest stroke ended less than `settle` ms ago (the Board: 2,500) is left out of the plan, answers included, and drawn as raw ink; a timer replans when it has rested (`Pad._replan`). Strokes restored from storage carry no time and count as old.
- The first training file (7 Nov, his tablet; `test/fixtures/training_2026-11-07.json`, `test/solve_check.js` §21) brought three reading rules: an **open 4** — an "L" with the upright drawn a little to its right, not touching, which the join model took for two symbols (c 1, L 1, ( 1) — is checked on its shape and joined when the classifier sees a 4 in the pair (`Recognizer.openFour`: the join probability is lifted to 0.75 and the pair's junk share capped at 0.1); a **tiny ring** between digits or letters, under 0.3 of their height, is a dot (`fixRow`), so a times dot drawn as a little loop is no longer a 0; and **three 1s of one height** are 111, not |1| (`fixBars`). Composed set 90.1 → 90.6%, real school set unchanged. Also from his writing: a finger's tap within 30 s of the pen is the heel of the hand and is dropped (`Pad._finish`), and a root's index may sit lower when it is clearly left of the tick.
- Letter/number look-alikes without context: `q`/`9`, `s`/`5`, `z`/`2`, `b`/`6`, `P`/`p`, `V`/`v`. The school prior leans toward numbers; with `symbols: 'all'` pass `prior: 0`.
- Some two-stroke `4`s are still split into `1 +`; some small radicals read as `r`/`v`.
- Subscripts are deliberately hard to trigger (rare in middle school); `\lim`, `\sum` and `\int` limits are not laid out.
- A late stroke that does not touch its symbol (the two halves of an x written far apart in time and not meeting) is still read on its own.
- `%` and `$` are learned from synthetic samples only (CROHME has neither).
- The views draw what was read: a misread symbol is tidied, re-written or typeset as what it was read as (a correction redraws it). Answers are worked out from what was read, so a misread symbol gives a wrong (gold) answer until it is fixed; the answer itself is never wrong for what was read (`test/solve_check.js` §12).
- Solve does not solve cubics or higher (beyond x³ = q), quadratics with π or another approximation in their coefficients (beyond x² = p), quadratic inequalities, rearranging with π in it (C = 2πr, r =), with the letter squared or underneath (A = πr², r =), or with a bottom of several terms that might cancel; a square root that does not come out in x² = p is still a cut decimal (±7.071…) while a full quadratic gives a radical ((−3 ± √5)/2), which is a deliberate inconsistency kept from 1.4. Two equations beside each other that are the same line (x + y = 1, 2x + 2y = 2) answer "x =" with −y + 1 (true of both) rather than nothing. Repeating decimals are read only from LaTeX (the reader makes no bar-over-digits node), and subscripted letters (x₁, y₂) only when the reader takes the subscript, which it rarely does. The letter `l` (A = lw, P = 2l + 2w) is usually read as e, 1 or 2, so those formulas are answered for what was read. It does not solve equations written far from their "x =" (a board keeps each cluster of writing to itself); it does no units or money and evaluates no functions. An answer that would run off the right edge of the board is squeezed to fit after its "=" (to no less than 0.6 of the writing), or else written under its line at full size, ending at the edge (`Pad._fitAnswers`; laid out again when the board changes size); it does not look for other writing in the way. Limits of `\sum`, `\int` and `\lim` are not laid out, so they are not either.
- My hand uses one drawing of each character (the Replicator's average of the box and the sentences, where there are sentences), so repeated letters look identical. Fraction bars are the writer's minus sign stretched, brackets their own brackets made taller, and roots their `√` with the overline redrawn to cover the body; a character missing from the hand (or written in no Replicator box, like `\sum`) is drawn tidied.
- **Board input on real hardware.** The pointer rules were tested with synthetic pointer events and Chrome's emulated touch, not on the ActivPanel or the Tab S9+. Whether the panel reports a touch's `width`/`height` (the palm rule needs it), whether its pen arrives as `pen` or as `touch`, and what `buttons` its eraser end sends are unknown: the overlay on a long press of "Nib." shows exactly that, and the 28 px, 350 ms and 1 s thresholds may need tuning from it. Pen hover keeps touch ignored only where the pen sends hover events.
- **Reveal and fit edges.** A revealed answer is keyed to the strokes of its line and the answer: adding any stroke to the line hides it, and erasing one and undoing brings it back shown. `pad.answers` and Copy LaTeX include answers still behind their chips. Fit shrinks ink once per resize and never grows it back: turning a tablet to portrait and back leaves the writing smaller (Undo restores it). A page's undo history is lost on reload (the pages themselves are kept).
- **Rail at narrow widths.** The top or bottom rail is one row from 800 px up (labels go first, then the view names become icons below 1080 px); narrower than that it wraps to two rows. A side rail is one column of icons on screens 920 px or taller and two columns below that.
- CROHME is adults writing on tablets; children writing with fingers on Chromebooks will be messier (the stress sets imitate some of it). Collecting a few hundred consented, anonymous samples from the pad (strokes + the corrected LaTeX) and fine-tuning would be the biggest single improvement; the correction memory already captures part of this per device. The Board's training log (above) now collects them; no network has been fine-tuned on it yet.
