# Found on 15 November 2026

Two faults found while the worker pool was being prepared (`docs/plans/worker-pool.md`). Both are in 1.6.7 and in
1.7.0, neither has to do with the speed work, and **neither is fixed**: each fix changes what a pad reads, which is
Croix's to decide. Each has a probe in `tools/verify/` that exits 1 while the fault is there, to judge the fix by.

1. A page gets eight joins and no more.
2. A symbol whose strokes were moved apart is still read as what it was.

# 1. A page gets eight joins and no more

## What happens

A board reads its writing as pieces (the clusters `clustersOf` finds), then joins pieces that belong together but were
left apart by the space between them: a line that only asks (`x =`) under an equation, and the parts of one line with
a wide gap in it (`joinOperands`, `src/pad.js`). The read asks `joinOperands` again after each join, "at most a few
rounds":

```js
for (let round = 0; round < 8; round++) {          // _partsPass: the scheduled read and recognize() both run it
  const joined = joinOperands(parts); if (!joined) break;
  …
```

but `joinOperands` returns as soon as it has made **one** join. So a page gets eight joins a read, in a fixed order,
and the pairs after the eighth are never joined, however often the page is read. Their pieces are read apart: an
`x =` that is not joined to its equation is not answered; the right half of a line with a gap is its own expression.

It takes a crowded page. Up to eight such pairs nothing is lost, and writing that sits close enough is one cluster to
begin with and needs no join.

## How to see it

`node tools/verify/joins_probe.js` builds pages of school problems and, after the pad has read each, asks
`joinOperands` what it would still join. With Nib 1.7.0:

| Page | Clusters | Joined by the read | Left undone | Answered |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 12 lines with a gap each | 23 | 8 | 0 | |
| 20 lines with a gap each | 39 | 8 | 6 | |
| 40 lines with a gap each | 78 | 8 | 14 | |
| 12 equations, each asked `letter =` underneath | 19 | 6 | 0 | 10 |
| 20 equations, each asked `letter =` underneath | 32 | 8 | 3 | 16 |
| 40 equations, each asked `letter =` underneath | 52 | 8 | 8 | 22 |

(The probe's pages are made from the school sample by moving strokes, so a few of its `letter =` lines are misread for
other reasons; the column that matters is "left undone". It exits 1 while any page is left with joins undone.)

## The fix, when it is wanted

Every join makes one piece out of two, so the loop ends by itself after at most `pieces − 1` rounds: the bound can be
the number of pieces instead of 8, or `joinOperands` can make every join of a round at once. Either way:

- it changes readings on pages with more than eight such pairs, on purpose, so the scripted sessions' references move
  where they have such pages (`big7` and `big8` in `tools/verify/gold-1.6.7/sessions/` have some 20 and 12 lines
  with a gap): a new reference is recorded and the differences are read one by one;
- a join costs a read of the joined piece (about eight classifications each on the sample page), so a page of forty
  joins reads some 300 groups more than today — which is one more reason to do it before the worker pool is built,
  and to let the pool take the joined pieces too (`docs/plans/worker-pool.md`, "After the first build");
- `joins_probe.js` must then end `every page was joined to the end`.

# 2. A symbol whose strokes were moved apart is still read as what it was

## What happens

A recognizer keeps what it has classified: each group of strokes under the strokes' ids and the size it was read at
(`Recognizer.cache`), and each pair of neighbouring strokes' chance of being one symbol under their ids
(`Recognizer.jcache`). A stroke keeps its id for as long as it is the same object, and the select tool moves a stroke
in place. `Pad._inkMoved` clears the pad's readings so the ink is read again — "ink that moved is read again where it
is" — but only ink that was made bigger or smaller gets new recognizers. So when **one stroke of a symbol is moved
and the other is not**, the pair is still in the cache as it was before the move, and the read that follows finds it
there.

Ink moved as a whole (a selection of whole expressions, which is what moving is mostly used for) is not touched by
this: its strokes keep their places relative to each other, and the old classification is still the right one.

## How to see it

`node tools/verify/moved_probe.js`: "2 + 3" with the plus written as two strokes; the bar of the plus is selected and
nudged; the pad reads again, and a recognizer that has never seen the strokes reads the same ink.

| The bar of the plus | The pad reads | The same ink, read fresh |
| --- | --- | --- |
| nudged down by 26 | `2+3` | `21-3` |
| nudged down by 40 (clear of the stroke it crossed) | `2+3` | `213` over `-` |
| nudged up by 30 | `2+3` | `2T3` |

The same on a board and on a pad of one expression, in 1.6.7 and 1.7.0. It lasts until the page is opened again (new
recognizers) or the ink is resized. The case that would be met in a lesson: two bars written a little too far apart
to be an equals sign, one of them dragged closer — the pair was classified as it stood, and stays so.

## The fix, when it is wanted

A stroke that moved must not be found under its old id: in `_inkMoved`, the moved strokes are forgotten by the
recognizers (their entry in `Recognizer.ids`), so every group and pair with one of them in it is classified again and
everything else on the page stays cached. A selection moved as a whole is then classified again too, which costs a
read of its strokes (about 1.7 classifications a stroke: nothing for an expression, two seconds for a full page
dragged at once on this machine); if that matters, ids can be kept when every stroke of a recognizer's piece moved by
the same amount. `moved_probe.js` must then end `moved ink is read where it is`. The scripted sessions move whole pieces only, so
their readings should stay as they are (`tools/verify/sessions.sh` says).

The worker pool does not depend on this and does not make it worse in kind (an answer from a worker is the
classification of the strokes as they were when it was asked for, as a classification made on the page's thread
before a move is); but the pool's rule for late answers is written with this fix in mind: answers are stored under
the ids the strokes had when they were asked for.
